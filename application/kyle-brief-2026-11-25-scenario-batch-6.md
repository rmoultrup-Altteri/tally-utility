Kyle, batch 6: money we can't match to a customer yet, and pledges from assistance agencies. 6 questions and a figures check, most important first; each says what we've written, so "yes" is enough where we have it right. The worked cases are in `scenarios/unmatched-cash-and-pledges.md`. Where a tariff or agreement is silent we've used your rule (follow the tariff; the platform default only where it's silent), and each utility must state its held-money settings and its pledge rule before it goes live, so we only ask what decides whether a customer can be cut off or charged, or turns on Texas law. Two things you should know first. (1) Money nobody can match is held apart from every customer until an operator identifies it; it's never posted on a guess, even when the amount matches someone's bill exactly. (2) Gulf Plains (investor-owned) is bound by 16 TAC §7.460(b)(2): no disconnection of a delinquent residential customer "for a billing period in which the provider receives a written pledge … that it is forwarding sufficient payment to continue service". The city systems aren't bound by it, so for them each city states its own pledge rule before go-live.

1. (SCN-0124) A CEAP pledge reaches Gulf Plains on Sep 11. We protect the customer for the billing period it arrives in, the month of service Sep 1 – 30 (read Oct 1, billed Oct 5), though the agency has 45 days, to Oct 25. If the agency hasn't paid by Oct 1, may Gulf Plains disconnect for the July bill, or does the agreement's "not delinquent if paid within 45 days" hold the pledged amount to Oct 25? (That agreement is between the agency and the utility, not a rule the customer holds.) And is "the billing period" the month of service the pledge arrives in, as we've written, or the period of the last bill issued? Also: does a $30.00 pledge against $47.80 delinquent count as "sufficient payment"? And a pledge by phone isn't "written", so it protects only once the letter arrives; does a pledge entered in the agency's web portal count as written?
2. (SCN-0120) Held money past the utility's handling limit (10 days in our example) stops a cut-off of any customer it might belong to. Within the first 10 days nothing stops it. Should a possible owner be protected from the day the money came?
3. (SCN-0125) An agency's payment posts, the pledge reads paid, then the check bounces after the pay-by date. The bill is delinquent again. Does the customer get a fresh disconnection notice before a cut-off resumes?
4. (SCN-0124) The vendor agreement keeps service on for up to five business days while the agency checks eligibility, before any pledge exists. Nothing records "the agency is checking this customer", since a pledge needs an amount. How do utilities learn an agency is checking a customer, and what holds the cut-off in those days?
5. (SCN-0120) Pecan Flat adds a $20 service charge when a bill is unpaid at 1:00 p.m. on the 10th, and cuts service off. If the cut-off is held because of unidentified money, is the $20 still added? Does it go with being unpaid at that hour, or with the cut-off itself?
6. (SCN-0127) An agency overpays its pledge and the extra is left over. A leftover becomes a credit for later bills only with approval: the customer's approval or the agency's? (A refund of it always goes to the agency, by 10 TAC §6.312(f).)
