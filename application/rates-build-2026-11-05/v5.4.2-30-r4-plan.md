## Blocking

### B1. A past read can answer one place twice, or both within and outside (Codex B1, executed by the author with its probe; Fable S1)
-16 forbids two live memberships of one place on one date. A void and a re-record by overlapping transactions leave both "known" at an instant between them. r3 checked only the exclusive-group rule, and counted rows, so:
- a district (no group) recorded again came back twice, and a place corrected from within to outside came back as both, with no error (Codex);
- a county recorded again was refused as "two places" although there is one (Fable).

**Fix:** the live set is read once. One place (its kind and code: a successor row is the same place, -16) with more than one row, relation or distance refuses (21000, "more than one answer for one place"). The same place recorded twice alike is one answer (`DISTINCT`). The exclusive-group check counts places. The refusal is not extended to the agreeing case, as Codex's fix would: that answer is certain, and a refusal there would make the bill's instant unreadable for good. Codex's reason against a bare `DISTINCT` (it cannot settle opposite relations or distances) is met by the refusal before it.
- Added by the author: two successive rows of one district, both known at the instant (the place's close recorded later), answer once per row in r3; refused too (M12g).
- Tests: battery M12d–M12g, races R6 (agreeing: one answer) and R7 (opposite: refused) with two real sessions; mutations N57–N62, N64, N65.

[a read that can be ambiguous refuses, never picks; one that is not ambiguous answers]
