Audited 2026-11-02, read-only, against `rulings-digest.md` (same folder) and the standing rules. Source: `gas-billing-memory/application/` at GBM HEAD (last commit 2026-11-01).

Method: I read every workflow's header (goal, trigger, pending decisions) and its open-question headlines. I grepped all 80 files for dollar figures, Texas statute references and the terms the digest reverses: stored counters, a "refunded" status, the stored running balance, the retired allocation settings, `pending_dispute`, stored late-fee flags, the occupant column on a location, the deferred gas-cost balance and true-up, unbilled revenue, and §7.45. ## Part 1 — the 80 workflows (`configurable-rules/workflows/`)

### What these files are, and why that matters for every verdict

The workflows were written in July and August 2026, in "WU6 Session 3A–3M" plus a coda dated 2026-08-11. They come in two styles:

- **Domains 1–4 (meters, reads, rates, gas cost): 23 short files, 3–7 KB.** They describe use cases: a main path, alternative and exception paths, state changes, and a test note. Few of them name table columns. Most have "Pending decisions: none".
- **Domains 5–13 (everything else): 57 long files, 8–23 KB.** Each runs the same use-case skeleton, then spends half its length on **gaps in the schema as it stood before v5.4.2**. Their open questions are worded as table findings, for example "nothing maintains `payments.applied_amount`", "`service_locations.customer_id` is the occupant" or "no holiday calendar exists". Many of these gaps have since been closed or reshaped by a ruling or a patch. The rest are still open, but are worded in table terms.

Three points hold across the set:

1. **They describe tables, not rules.** The standing rule is "scenarios describe rules and rulings, not tables". The long files' open-question and state-transition sections are reviews of the old schema, and none of them can be reused as a scenario as written. The parts worth keeping are the **main path, the alternative and exception paths, and the test note**, which name the failures that cost money.
2. **There are almost no worked numbers.** 58 of the 80 files contain no dollar figure at all. The others carry made-up figures that illustrate a test idea ("dispute $180 of a $600 bill", "eight $500 invoices under a $1,000 limit", "write off $800, recover $300"). **No file works a bill through to the cent.** The one file that comes close, meter-accuracy, quotes a Texas rule's $15 fee and 2% tolerance as if every utility had them.
3. **Many files treat 16 TAC §7.45 (Texas's customer-service rule for regulated gas utilities) as binding on the customer.** About 25 files cite §7.45, §7.460 or "RRC filing", and most read it as the rule that applies. That is wrong for our first customers, the city-owned systems, which are outside GURA: their policy is their own tariff rows, with no assumed defaults (Kyle K-07, 10-15). Every such file needs its "Texas requires…" wording changed to "the utility's tariff row says…, with the Texas law row kept as reference for systems regulated by the Railroad Commission". The digest also notes one statute that *does* bind city systems: the due-date delay for customers aged 60 and over in Utilities Code §182.002 (W3 "for Kyle", pending Kyle). No workflow mentions it.

**Verdict scale used below:** *keep*: usable as written. *Revise light*: a few sentences or one path contradict a ruling. *Revise heavy*: the flow skeleton survives, but its rule source, its open questions and its state changes must be rewritten. *Rewrite*: the core mechanism has been replaced by a ruling. *Obsolete*: a ruling took it out of the platform's scope.

### The four questions asked

**1. Does the cast assume investor-owned or GURA-regulated utilities rather than small city-owned systems?** Yes, in structure, though not in name.
- Three of the six tenants are city systems, but **the two small ones (Marfa, Brenham), the ones that look like our first customers, are empty shells**: no rate schedules, no customers, no locations, no meters. The rate-schedule file says they "reuse the simple shape… if/when they host customers".
- **Every worked-out customer lives under the investor-owned utility (4), the two co-ops (4), or "Lubbock Municipal Gas" (3).** Lubbock is an ~85k-meter city system with a **GRIP rider (Utilities Code §104.301)**. GRIP is a GURA mechanism for regulated utilities, so it is wrong for a city system. The file also calls Lubbock "the largest standalone city system in Texas". I believe Lubbock and San Angelo are actually served by Atmos (an investor-owned utility), and CPS Energy (San Antonio, city-owned) is far larger. **Unverified; check the market map.**
- **FIX-JUR-001 applies one Texas parameter set to every tenant**: §7.45 deposits (1/6 cap, mandatory waivers, 30-day interest, refund after 12 clean bills), §7.45 per-cause backbilling caps, §7.45 disconnect clocks in working days, a 15-day minimum due date, bilingual notices, and §7.460 extreme weather. The Brenham tenant's disconnect posture says "standard §7.45 working-day clocks", and Lone Star's 16-day terms are "near the §7.45 15-day floor". This breaks K-07: for city systems these are tariff rows with no defaults. The set also leaves out the one statute the digest says *does* bind city systems, the due-date delay for customers aged 60 and over (§182.002).
- The rate-schedule naming (RS-1 for unincorporated and RS-1-INC for incorporated, with "regulatory_authority = RRC" on the unincorporated variant) copies an investor-owned utility's tariff (the strategy names "Texas Gas Service-style"). For a city system, customers outside city limits are still on the city's own tariff. Whether the Railroad Commission has any say there is a fact to check with Kyle; the fixtures assume it does.
- Tenant settings use the retired allocation knobs (`payment_allocation_strategy`, `overpayment_handling = hold_as_credit` without the customer's approval; K-05 and R-06), and "intended" late-fee and disconnect postures are hard-coded as defaults. That conflicts with "no assumed defaults".

**2. Is the cast Texas-only by design?** Yes, explicitly. The strategy says "Launch scope: Texas only… Multi-state expansion is deferred… a v2 concern". The README says "synthetic, **Texas-only**". Every fixture ID is prefixed TX-. The parameter set sits on one "TX-RRC" record instead of dated law rows per jurisdiction, each with a citation. That breaks the standing rule (Texas first, never hard-code Texas). The fix is mechanical: split FIX-JUR-001 into dated law rows with citations, and keep a second state's rows as a test of non-Texas data (the digest's Florida deposit example would do). The structure (tenant → city → location) does not need to change.

**3. Is the cast usable now as the named people and places for worked bills?** **Not as it stands.** It would work as a seed for names and IDs.
- **There are no prices.** No volumetric rates, no gas-cost factor values, no pipeline safety fee, no taxability declared per line. Kyle Q5 requires every charge to declare its tax status. A worked bill cannot be computed from these files.
- Tax: it has one made-up city rate per city, but **a tax jurisdiction is a place, and a location links to every jurisdiction it sits in** (city, county, transit, special district) (Kyle Q1, 10-14). The fixtures carry no county or transit layer. Residential exemption is modelled as a city-wide 0% rather than as the item and the customer both being taxable for that service type, with a $0.00 line for an exempt customer (Q5).
- Franchise fees: the base is "gross_revenue", with no excluded lines. Kyle Q2/Q3 (10-14) require a percentage of all billed lines **except excluded ones (the pipeline safety fee)**, **left out of the sales-tax base**, with the base type chosen per jurisdiction and **no default**. The 2% ceiling under §182.025 is still unconfirmed (DE-FX-1).
- Pipeline safety fee: "$1.00 per service line" is stated as a constant. K-04 (10-15) says it is a dated rule with no fixed cap; one 2024 tariff shows $1.22 per customer; confirm against §8.201.
- Locations and people: FIX-SL-012 stores "customer: owner · occupant: tenant" on the location, but occupancy is now a dated record and locations never change (R-10). `do_not_disconnect` is a single flag, while programs now keep a dated history (R-07). `pending_dispute` is a protective program, but a dispute now shelters an amount (W3 D7). There is no deceased-customer case (R-08). Meters are attached to a location without an install history (R-10), and the gas heat-content factor is a constant per meter rather than a dated zone set (K-02).
- **Hazard with real names: the fixture "Brenham Municipal Gas" has invented policy** (1%/month plus $3 late fee, 25-day grace, §7.45 clocks). The digest's dispute rule cites **Brenham's real tariff** (pay 100%, no cutoff during the investigation). A scenario that says "Brenham" would be ambiguous between the invented tenant and the real tariff row. The same goes for made-up tax rates attached to real cities (Marfa shown with 0% city tax, unverified).
