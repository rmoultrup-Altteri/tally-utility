# Audit 04: patches v5.4.2-01 … -06

## Summary
- **-01 and -02 (immutability, append-only, void_invoice):** pure mechanism. No jurisdiction rule is encoded, so there are no findings.
- **-06 (deposits) is the problem patch.** It encodes 16 TAC §7.45 almost entirely as structure:
  - a literal `upper(tenants.state) = 'TX'` branch for the residential cap;
  - the day-31 interest cliff with interest back to day 1;
  - simple interest on a 365-day basis, credited at refund;
  - the refund trigger of 12 clean bills and at most 2 delinquencies;
  - §7.45 waiver classes, additional-deposit triggers and deposit bases as closed CHECK lists.
- **Deposit interest rate (-06):** it is effective-dated data, which is good. But it is keyed per TENANT. The actual rule is a per-state published rate (in Texas the PUCT sets it every year), so it has no `state_code` or `service_type`.
- **-03 (bitemporal):** mostly mechanism, but it carries some Texas shapes:
  - `calc_owner = 'state_of_tx'`;
  - Texas Comptroller sales-tax exemption modelling (Form 01-339, Rule 3.287, §151.317, R-13 dropping 'industrial');
  - a city-name-keyed franchise-fee model;
  - an "RRC: no de minimis" rule baked into an unconditional anomaly;
  - closed lists for customer class and estimation method.
- **-05:** the consecutive-estimate cap is a tenant setting (default 3, counted in reads). The Texas six-month ceiling is explicitly not enforced, and there is no per-state statutory ceiling anywhere.
- **-04:** snapshot v1 is gas-centric (`gas_factors` and `wna_inputs` are mandatory sections). Its provenance whitelist cannot cite a future platform-held jurisdiction-rule table.
- **Good examples (class a):**
  - free-text `regulatory_authority` / `regulatory_code` / `tariff_number` on each schedule version;
  - tenant-held `regulatory_class` on rate items;
  - `jurisdictions[]` in the snapshot's `tax_inputs`;
  - the no-fallback, raise-when-missing lookup contract (`deposit_interest_rate_as_of`, the as_of family). This is the right mechanism; only the key is wrong.
- **Counts:** a = 3, b = 7, c = 17 (27 findings).

## Theme 1: Deposits (16 TAC §7.45; CI-125/129/130/131). File: v5.4.2-06-account-lifecycle-and-deposits.sql

### Texas residential cap: literal state branch
- where: v5.4.2-06:875-881, function `enforce_deposit()`
- rule: if `upper(tenants.state) = 'TX'`, the customer is residential, the deposit is cash and the basis is §7.45, then `cap_amount` is required. The cap is 1/6 of estimated annual billing (COMMENT on `deposits.cap_amount`, line 647). Source: 16 TAC §7.45, CI-129, table #53 rank 8.
- class: c
- other states: The cap formula differs by state. Many states cap at two months' (or 2/12 of annual) estimated or average billing, and some set a separate non-residential cap (a guess about which states do what). A state with no residential cap requirement also cannot say so. The rule also reads `tenants.state`, so it assumes one state per tenant. A utility serving customers across a state line cannot be expressed.
- fix: a platform table `deposit_rules(state_code, service_type, customer_class, effective_from/to, cap_basis enum(annual_fraction|months_of_billing|none), cap_factor, source_note)` with EXCLUDE no-overlap and a `deposit_rule_for(state, service_type, class, date)` lookup that raises when no row is in force. Take the state from the premise or service location, not the tenant. Seed Texas as 1/6 of annual. — size: M

### Cap columns shaped for the Texas formula
- where: v5.4.2-06:601-603, 626-628, 647 — `deposits.cap_amount`, `cap_basis_annual_billing`, `cap_binding`
- rule: the cap basis is stored only as "annual billing". Source: §7.45 (1/6 of annual).
- class: b
- other states: A "two highest months" or "average monthly bill × N" cap has no place to store its basis. `cap_basis_annual_billing` would hold the wrong quantity.
- fix: `cap_basis_amount` plus `cap_basis_kind` (annual_billing | avg_monthly | highest_months) and a `deposit_rule_id` FK to the row that produced the cap. — size: S

### Day-31 interest cliff (held > 30 days)
- where: v5.4.2-06:967-969, 974-976, 1041-1044 — `enforce_deposit_event()` in the interest_accrued and refunded branches
- rule: no accrual until `effective_on - posted_on >= 31`. A deposit exhausted or refunded within 30 days carries no interest. Source: CI-130, 16 TAC §7.45.
- class: c
- other states: Several states set the threshold in months, for example no interest unless held 6 or 12 months (a guess about which states). Some pay interest from day 1 with no threshold (a guess). A state may also require interest only once the deposit reaches a minimum amount.
- fix: add `interest_min_hold_days` (NULL means none) to the per-state deposit rule row, and have the guard read it through the lookup. — size: S

### Interest retroactive to the posting date
- where: v5.4.2-06:978-981 — `enforce_deposit_event()`, first-period rule
- rule: the first accrual period must start ON `posted_on`, retroactive to day 1. Source: CI-130, §7.45.
- class: c
- other states: A state whose interest runs only from the threshold date, or from the next anniversary, cannot be expressed (a guess that such states exist).
- fix: add `interest_retroactive_to_posting boolean` (or `accrual_start_rule` enum) to the deposit rule row. — size: S

### Simple interest, 365-day basis, cents rounding per period
- where: v5.4.2-06:549-555 — function `deposit_accrual_amount()`, IMMUTABLE. It is enforced exactly at 1005-1008.
- rule: `round(principal × rate × days/365, 2)` per period, simple, never compounded. Source: CI-125 and CI-130 as implemented.
- class: c
- other states: Some states require compounding annually, or require interest to be paid or credited to the bill each year rather than at refund. That is a guess for specific states, but annual crediting is a common PUC shape. A 360-day basis or leap-year handling cannot be expressed either.
- fix: add `interest_method` (simple | compound_annual), `day_count` (actual_365 | actual_actual) and `credit_cadence` (at_refund | annual | on_bill) to the deposit rule row. `deposit_accrual_amount` then takes the rule, not literals. — size: M

### Accrued interest must be credited in full before refund (credit timing)
- where: v5.4.2-06:1049-1051 — `enforce_deposit_event()`, refunded branch
- rule: interest is paid at refund. The only guard is "credited == accrued at refund", and nothing enforces a periodic credit obligation. Source: CI-130 / CI-131.
- class: c
- other states: In a state that requires interest to be credited annually while the deposit is held, the ledger cannot flag an overdue annual credit.
- fix: drive a periodic-credit obligation (a view like `deposits_refund_due`) from the rule row's `credit_cadence`. — size: S

### Deposit interest rate keyed per tenant, not per state
- where: v5.4.2-06:482-496 (table `deposit_interest_rates`), 525 (COMMENT: "the PUCT-set rate for a Texas tenant"), 527-547 (`deposit_interest_rate_as_of(tenant, date)`)
- rule: an annual deposit interest rate, effective-dated and append-only, with no fallback. Source: Kyle 2026-06-12, CI-125. Texas: the PUCT sets it every year (Utilities Code §183.003).
- class: b. The mechanism is right (effective-dated, raises when missing); the key is wrong.
- other states: The rate is statewide law in Texas, so every Texas tenant must re-enter the same row. Other states set the rate by statute or PUC order, sometimes tied to a Treasury yield (a guess for specific states). There is no `state_code`, no `service_type`, no `source_note`, and no EXCLUDE (only UNIQUE on tenant and date). A tenant-specific tariff override is legitimately per-tenant, but it should be a second layer.
- fix: a platform table `deposit_interest_rates(state_code, service_type, effective_from, effective_to, annual_rate, source_note)` with EXCLUDE, plus an optional tenant override table. `deposit_interest_rate_as_of(state, service_type, date)` raises when no row is in force. Move the backdating guard over too. — size: M

### Refund trigger: 12 clean bills, ≤ 2 delinquencies, not currently delinquent
- where: v5.4.2-06:790-818 — function `deposit_refund_trigger_state()`. The constants are at 815-817 (`>= 12`, `<= 2`).
- rule: the mandatory refund trigger for a §7.45 deposit. Source: CI-131, 16 TAC §7.45.
- class: c
- other states: The count differs by state and class, for example 12 months residential versus 24 months non-residential (a guess about which states). Some states measure months of service, not bills. Some count disconnections for nonpayment as disqualifying.
- fix: add `refund_after_clean_bills`, `refund_max_delinquencies`, `refund_measure` (bills | months) and `disqualify_on_disconnect` to the per-state deposit rule row (by customer class), and have the function read them. — size: S

### deposits_refund_due view: §7.45 bases and account-closure trigger hardcoded
- where: v5.4.2-06:821-833 — view `deposits_refund_due`
- rule: only cash deposits whose basis is IN ('credit_evaluation', 'additional_trigger', 'tariff') appear. A customer status of closed, final_billed or inactive fires the refund. Source: CI-131, table #54 rule 3.
- class: c
- other states: Which bases carry a mandatory refund is §7.45's taxonomy. A state that requires refunds of non-cash guarantees (release obligations) or has other bases cannot be added without editing the view.
- fix: move "refund-mandatory" to a flag on a platform `deposit_bases(state_code, basis_code, refund_mandatory, interest_bearing, waivable)` table. The view then joins it. — size: M

### deposits.basis closed list: §7.45 categories plus federal §366
- where: v5.4.2-06:621 (CHECK `deposits_basis_check`) and 865 (`enforce_deposit`: `basis IN ('credit_evaluation', 'additional_trigger', 'tariff')` is treated as "§7.45-basis")
- rule: basis ∈ credit_evaluation | adequate_assurance_366 | additional_trigger | tariff | legacy_unknown. The code equates three of them with §7.45. `adequate_assurance_366` is federal bankruptcy law (11 USC §366), which is fine nationally.
- class: c
- other states: State deposit bases differ, for example "prior-account unpaid balance", "no credit history" or "high-risk commercial" (a guess). The code's "is this a state-law deposit" branch is a hardcoded IN list.
- fix: a platform `deposit_bases` table (per state, effective-dated) with flags `state_regulated`, `waivable`, `refund_mandatory`. Keep `adequate_assurance_366` as a platform-wide row. — size: M

### Additional-deposit triggers: closed §7.45 list
- where: v5.4.2-06:622-623 — CHECK `deposits_trigger_basis_check`
- rule: trigger_basis ∈ nsf | disconnect_history | broken_dpa. Source: §7.45 additional-deposit triggers, table #55.
- class: c
- other states: Other states name other triggers, for example "diversion or theft of service", "late payment in N of the last 12 months" or "change in use" (a guess). The list cannot grow per state.
- fix: fold into the per-state `deposit_bases` or `deposit_triggers` rows. — size: S

### Deposit-waiver classes: Texas §7.45 categories
- where: v5.4.2-06:575-577 (CHECK `deposit_waiver_determinations_class_check` and the certification CHECK), 586 (COMMENT: "Texas Council on Family Violence reference required"), 865-874 (`enforce_deposit`: any in-force waiver blocks any §7.45-basis deposit)
- rule: waiver_class ∈ family_violence_certified | age_65_no_balance | good_payment_history | tariff. The family-violence class requires a certification reference. Source: CI-129, 16 TAC §7.45(5)(C), Kyle Q-6.
- class: c
- other states: Common non-Texas waiver classes include LIHEAP or low-income recipients, medical certification, military deployment, and prior-utility letter of credit (a guess for specific states). Age thresholds vary (60 vs 65; a guess). A state may waive only for residential customers, or only partially. The blocking logic also assumes "a waiver blocks every state-basis deposit".
- fix: a platform `deposit_waiver_classes(state_code, service_type, class_code, requires_certification, certifying_body_note, customer_classes[], effective_from/to, source_note)` table, and an FK from determinations to that row. The guard reads which bases the class blocks. — size: M

### Accepted deposit instruments: federal §366 list as a closed CHECK
- where: v5.4.2-06:624 (CHECK `deposits_instrument_check`) and 964-966 (only cash accrues interest)
- rule: instrument ∈ cash | letter_of_credit | certificate_of_deposit | surety_bond | prepayment | guarantor | other_agreed. Only cash bears interest. Source: 11 USC §366(c)(1)(A) plus guarantor, table #55. The patch defers per-tariff acceptance (header lines 138-140).
- class: c (low severity: `other_agreed` is an escape hatch)
- other states: Some states require interest on a CD or prepayment held by the utility, and some mandate that a third-party guarantor letter be accepted for residential customers (a guess). "Only cash accrues interest" is a statutory reading, not a universal rule.
- fix: add an `interest_bearing_instruments[]` / `accepted_instruments[]` pair to the per-state deposit rule row. — size: S

### Sales-tax exemption model is Texas Comptroller-shaped
- where:
  - v5.4.2-03:1439-1441 — CHECK `customer_tax_exemptions_exemption_type_check`
  - v5.4.2-03:188-198 — precondition raise citing "Comptroller Rule 3.287"

  - v5.4.2-03 header 48-50 — R-13 removed 'industrial'
- rule: only certificate-backed exemptions are rows. Residential exemption is assumed to follow from rate class (Texas Tax Code §151.317). Predominant-use (manufacturing) exemptions are removed from the domain because of Texas practice. Verification-before-active is justified by Texas seller liability. Source: R-12, R-13, Comptroller Rule 3.287.
- class: c
- other states: In other states the residential exemption is partial or county-optional, or residential utility gas is taxed (a guess for specific states). Manufacturing or industrial exemptions are certificate rows in some states. The exemption_type list (non_profit | government | agricultural | sales_for_resale | religious | educational | medical | other) is a single platform domain with no state key. `should_charge_tax()` (1795-1832) is binary, taxable or exempt, with no partial percentage.
- fix: a platform `tax_exemption_types(state_code, code, certificate_form, partial_allowed, effective_from/to, source_note)` table, with `customer_tax_exemptions.exemption_type` referencing it. Add `exempt_fraction`. Move the "residential derived from rate class" rule into per-state data. — size: M

### Franchise fees keyed by (tenant, city_name text)
- where: v5.4.2-03:1396-1397 (EXCLUDE `franchise_fee_rules_open_no_overlap_excl` on tenant_id, city_name, dates), 1724 (`franchise_fee_as_of(p_tenant_id, p_city_name, …)`), 519 (`rate_schedule_versions.franchise_city text`)
- rule: one franchise fee rule per tenant per city name, following the Texas municipal franchise model. The underlying table comes from tu.sql; -03 re-keys its uniqueness.
- class: b
- other states: Fees or utility taxes can be levied by county, township, school district or special district, and can stack (city plus county). Some are per-unit (per therm or per kWh) rather than a percentage of revenue, for example Illinois municipal utility taxes (a guess on specifics). A bare city-name text has no state, so "Springfield" is ambiguous across states. Only one rule per city means stacked levies at one place cannot be expressed.
- fix: key by a jurisdiction FK (place with state, type city|county|district) and add `levy_kind`, so several levies per premise are possible. Tenant-held rows are fine because the fee is a franchise agreement. — size: M

### Unconditional correction review with a hardcoded "RRC: no de minimis" rule

## Theme 4: Read estimation and meter master. File: v5.4.2-05-read-and-bill-exception-substrate.sql

### Consecutive-estimate cap: tenant setting, count-based, no statutory ceiling
- where: v5.4.2-05:243-265 (`max_consecutive_estimates()`: `tenants.settings.estimation.max_consecutive_estimates`, DEFAULT 3, ≥ 1; COMMENT: "The Texas 6-month ceiling is NOT enforced here"); header 112-113 (the Texas cap CHECK is deferred to Kyle)
- rule: CI-113 / D14-1 — a cap on consecutive estimated reads per meter.
- class: b
- other states: State rules limit estimation differently. Some limit consecutive estimates by count (for example, no more than 2 in a row) and others by months without an actual read (for example, an actual read at least every 6 or 12 months). Pennsylvania-style actual-read-frequency rules are a guess on specifics. The count-only JSON setting cannot express a months-based limit. The statutory ceiling has no home, so a tenant could set a cap above the law.
- fix: a platform `estimation_limits(state_code, service_type, limit_kind count|months, limit_value, effective_from/to, source_note)` table with EXCLUDE and a raising lookup. The tenant setting stays as the utility's stricter choice, validated as ≤ the statutory limit. — size: M

- where: v5.4.2-05:398-426 — function `meter_master_incomplete_reasons()`
- rule: for service_type 'gas', `meter_pressure_class` is required, and when it is 'elevated' so is `meter_factor`. Source: CI-023.
- class: c (low severity)
- other states / services: Which attributes the volume-to-energy formula needs depends on the service type and on how the state or tariff converts volume (for example, heating-value or pressure-base requirements in therm-billing states). Electric CT/PT ratio completeness is not expressed (a guess: `multiplier` NOT NULL may cover it).
- fix: a per-service-type (optionally per-state) required-attribute list read by the function. — size: S

## Not findings (checked)
- **-01 and -02:** every CHECK and trigger there is lifecycle mechanism: invoice issuance, void, ledger append-only, payment and credit terminal states. None of them encodes a jurisdiction rule.
- **-05 `read_validation_exceptions.rule_code`, `invoice_exceptions.criterion`, queue and escalation enums:** these are CI-112/115 workflow categories, not statute.
- **-06 `customers.status` lifecycle** (closed reopens only to active; closed is refused while a deposit is open), `customer_attribute_history`, and the `account_ledger` type extensions: all generic mechanism.
- **-03 `service_type` lists (559, 1083):** a platform multi-service domain, not a Texas assumption (gas, water, electric, sewer and so on are all present).
