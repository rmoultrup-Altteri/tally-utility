# Audit 05 — patches v5.4.2-07 … -11 (surcharges, tax bases, exemptions, snapshot binding, definer hygiene)

## Summary
- **-07/-08 (regulatory surcharge riders) are built around one Texas statute, 16 TAC §8.201's Pipeline Safety Fee.** The closed `surcharge_kind` list, the PSF shape CHECK, the one-PSF-per-tenant EXCLUDE, the gas-only check, the `is_state_agency` boolean (the column comment says "agency of the State of Texas") and the America/Chicago day boundary are all hardcoded structure. The rules table is keyed by tenant and rate item, with no state, jurisdiction or service type.
- **Good:** no dollar figure is hardcoded. The cap is a configured amount, the $1.00 vs $0.50 question is left open, and the remittance gate is operator guidance, not structure (D4-1).
- **The rule shapes are §8.201's.** "Excluded from ALL tax bases" is a single boolean. The cap is an aggregate per meter per cycle of bill dates. Another state's assessment that is excluded from only some bases, or capped per bill, cannot be expressed.
- **-09 (exemptions):** the evidence gate and renewal queue are generic mechanisms (good). But the exemption category list is repeated as a literal in a function. The certificate-required designation, which is state law, lives per tenant in `tenants.settings` with no state or effective date. Its default and its error text cite Texas Comptroller Rule 3.287 and Form 01-339.
- **-10 (snapshot binding):** the v1 snapshot contract requires `gas_factors` (PGA, BTU, pressure, temperature) and WNA/HDD keys on every bill, so the snapshot is gas-only. An ordinary bill is priced strictly at `period_end`, which is a tariff/jurisdiction proration rule frozen in a trigger. The "historical" correction default is labelled RRC-compliant.
- **-11 (definer hygiene):** mechanism only. The one rule-bearing item is `get_correction_rate_date`'s hardcoded fallback to "historical", counted with the -10 finding. The `anomalies.entity_type` list is an entity domain, not law.
- **Counts:** 4 class a, 7 class b, 13 class c.

## Theme 1 — Regulatory surcharge riders (v5.4.2-07 / -08)

### surcharge_kind closed list
- where: v5.4.2-07:291 — CHECK `regulatory_surcharge_rules_kind_check` on `regulatory_surcharge_rules.surcharge_kind`
- rule: kinds = `pipeline_safety_fee` | `other_regulatory_cost_recovery` — source: CI-038, 16 TAC §8.201
- class: c
- other states: this is a two-value taxonomy with one Texas statute named. Other regulatory cost-recovery lines are all lumped into "other", which carries no behaviour. Examples: Florida's PSC regulatory assessment fee and gross-receipts pass-throughs (likely, not verified), state energy-assistance or universal-service surcharges on gas or electric, and water regulatory assessment fees (guess). Any kind needing its own required shape (below) cannot get one without a DDL change.
- fix: platform table `regulatory_surcharge_kinds` (state_code, service_type, kind_code, effective_from/to, source_note, plus the shape attributes below). The tenant rule gets an FK to a kind row instead of the CHECK. — size: M

### PSF shape CHECK
- where: v5.4.2-07:294 — CHECK `regulatory_surcharge_rules_psf_shape_check`
- rule: a `pipeline_safety_fee` must have `cap_per_service` NOT NULL, `excluded_from_tax_bases` = true and `exempts_state_agencies` = true — source: CI-038, 16 TAC §8.201(b)
- class: c
- other states: the statutory properties of Texas's fee are welded to the kind's name. A pipeline-safety recovery in another state (guess: Oklahoma, Louisiana or New Mexico, if any passes it through per customer) might be uncapped, taxable, or not exempt for state agencies. It could not use this kind and would fall into "other", losing its identity.
- fix: move "requires cap / requires exclusion / requires exemption" onto the per-jurisdiction kind row. A generic trigger checks the tenant rule against its kind row. — size: M

### One PSF per tenant EXCLUDE
- where: v5.4.2-07:309-311 — EXCLUDE `regulatory_surcharge_rules_one_psf_per_tenant_excl`
- rule: at most one open pipeline_safety_fee rule per tenant per bill date — source: Fable MEDIUM-2a, reading of §8.201 (one assessment)
- class: c
- other states: this assumes a tenant operates under one state's fee. A multi-state LDC, or a tenant with gas systems regulated by two states, legitimately has two pipeline-safety assessments over the same dates, and this refuses the second.
- fix: key the exclusion on (tenant, jurisdiction/state_code, kind) once rules carry a jurisdiction. — size: S (after the jurisdiction column exists)

### PSF gas-only service-type check
- where: v5.4.2-07:373-381 (re-issued v5.4.2-08:342-351) — function `enforce_regulatory_surcharge_rule()`
- rule: a pipeline_safety_fee rider's open versions must be service_type `gas` or `all` (literal `NOT IN ('gas','all')`) — source: 16 TAC §8.201 (gas-distribution assessment)
- class: c
- other states: "which service types a surcharge kind applies to" is statute data. A hazardous-liquid or propane pipeline-safety recovery, or a water or electric regulatory assessment, needs a different service-type set.
- fix: `applies_to_service_types` on the kind row. The trigger compares against it instead of a literal. — size: S

### Rules table keyed by tenant/rate item only; statutory flags live on tenant rows
- where: v5.4.2-07:259-300 — table `regulatory_surcharge_rules`
- rule: `excluded_from_tax_bases` and `exempts_state_agencies` are statutory facts, but the tenant sets them per rider. `regulatory_reference` is free text. There is no `state_code`, `service_type` or jurisdiction column. The per-kind statutory ceiling on `cap_per_service` is explicitly deferred (header lines 138-139). — source: CI-038 / D4-1
- class: b
- other states: the tenant-set cap is fine (it is the utility's tariff figure). But the statute's facts (exclusions, exempt classes, statutory ceiling) are re-declared by each tenant, can drift from law, and cannot differ by state for one tenant. When the ceiling lands it must not become a CHECK literal.
- fix: split into a platform kind row per (state_code, service_type, effective_from/to) with a no-overlap EXCLUDE, a `source_note` and the statutory ceiling (the meter_accuracy_thresholds model), plus a tenant rule referencing it. A generic check requires tenant cap <= statutory ceiling in force on the bill date, raising when no kind row is in force. — size: M

### Cap shape: aggregate per meter per cycle, positive lines only
- where: v5.4.2-07:851-878 (re-issued v5.4.2-08:222-250) — function `assert_surcharge_line()`; also comment on `invoice_line_items.meter_id` at -07:1194
- rule: cap = sum of positive amounts per (rate item, meter) over the rule's cycle, drafts counted; "per service" = per meter — source: §8.201 "total billed per service", drafting decisions at -07:160-171
- class: b
- other states: only one cap basis can be expressed. Another assessment might be capped per bill, per month, per customer account, per premise, or as a percentage of the bill. None of these fits `cap_per_service` + meter aggregation (guesses as to specific states).
- fix: a `cap_basis` attribute on the kind row (per_service_per_cycle | per_bill | per_account_per_period | pct_of_bill …) read by a general cap function. The current logic becomes the `per_service_per_cycle` branch. — size: M

### Assessment cycle = bracket of bill dates
- where: v5.4.2-07:264-265, 330-331 (columns `cycle_start` / `cycle_end`), decision at -07:155-159; enforced by v5.4.2-08:362-392 `enforce_invoice_date_rule_membership()` and the cycle-shrink / retract guards at -08:272-322
- rule: a rule governs lines by `invoices.invoice_date`, following §8.201's "billing cycle or cycles following remittance" — source: §8.201, D4-1
- class: b
- other states: an assessment defined on service rendered (service period), or on a calendar or fiscal year of consumption, cannot be bracketed by bill date without mis-attributing lines that straddle a boundary.
- fix: a `cycle_basis` attribute (bill_date | service_period_end) on the kind row, with the -07/-08 cycle membership checks parameterised on it. — size: S–M

### excluded_from_tax_bases is all-or-nothing
- where: v5.4.2-07:267, 333 (column comment), enforced at -07:746-752, -07:834-845, -07:1015-1023
- rule: one boolean means "not a base for ANY tax or percentage charge". The comment spells out the Texas triple: franchise fee, gas-utility tax, sales tax. — source: 16 TAC §8.201, decision tables #23 and #26
- class: b
- other states: a surcharge could be excluded from state sales tax but included in a municipal franchise-fee or utility-users-tax base, or the reverse (plausible; no specific state verified). The boolean cannot say "excluded from X, included in Y".
- fix: an exclusion list per kind: kind × tax/fee type (joined to the tax-jurisdiction and franchise-fee types). The base guard checks the citing line's tax type against it. — size: M

### State-agency exemption as a boolean on customers
- where: v5.4.2-07:469-475 — column `customers.is_state_agency` + CHECK `customers_state_agency_is_government_check`; v5.4.2-07:268 `exempts_state_agencies`; customer_attribute_history list at -07:478-480, -07:583
- rule: the column comment says "the account is an agency of the State of Texas and is exempt from the Pipeline Safety Fee" — source: CI-038, §8.201
- class: c
- other states: the exempt class is jurisdiction-specific, and the boolean cannot say which state. A multi-state utility's customer could be a Texas agency billed in another state. Other exemptions key on federal-government customers (federal immunity from state levies, which is general law), municipalities, or low-income program enrollment.
- fix: exempt customer categories on the kind row (a list of category codes). The customer designation becomes a dated (customer, category, jurisdiction) row set, or a category attribute in the attribute history, instead of a Texas-named boolean. — size: M

### Day boundary pinned to America/Chicago
- where: v5.4.2-07:586-601 — function `customer_is_state_agency_as_of()` (`AT TIME ZONE 'America/Chicago'`)
- rule: the end of the billed period is midnight Central time, "the fee is a Texas assessment". El Paso's Mountain hour is accepted as an error. — source: Fable MEDIUM-1
- class: c
- other states: any non-Central jurisdiction (Mountain, Pacific, Eastern, and Arizona without DST) evaluates the exemption at the wrong instant.
- fix: time zone from the jurisdiction row (or the service location's time zone). The function takes it as data. — size: S

### A surcharge rider is never charge_type 'tax'
- where: v5.4.2-07:829-833 (re-issued v5.4.2-08:200-204) — `assert_surcharge_line()`
- rule: any line of a ruled rider with charge_type = 'tax' is refused — source: CI-038, D4-1 ("PSF is a rate-page line item")
- class: c
- other states: some regulatory recoveries are presented as taxes or government fees on the bill, for example gross-receipts or regulatory-assessment pass-throughs (Florida likely, not verified). Whether a kind is billed as a tax line is per-kind data.
- fix: a `billed_as` (rate_item | tax) attribute on the kind row, with the check reading it. — size: S

### Cap is a configured figure, not a CHECK literal (good)
- where: v5.4.2-07:266, 293, 332 — `cap_per_service` + `regulatory_surcharge_rules_cap_positive_check`
- rule: no $1.00 / $0.50 figure is hardcoded. The tenant declares its filed-tariff amount, and the statutory ceiling is left to a later ruling. — source: CI-038 vs D4-1 discrepancy
- class: a
- other states: already expressible per tenant. What is missing is only the per-jurisdiction ceiling (see "Rules table keyed by tenant/rate item only").
- fix: none beyond the kind-row ceiling above. — size: S

### Remittance gate / billing window kept out of structure (good)
- where: v5.4.2-07:26-30, 224-228 (header; `remitted_on` / `remitted_amount` removed); compliance report view at -07:1107-1145 carries no due-date constant (the "90 days" is only in the COMMENT)
- rule: "bill after remittance, never before" and the 90-day report are operator guidance — source: Kyle D4-1
- class: a
- other states: no Texas timing is frozen, so nothing to unwind.
- fix: none. If a remittance or report deadline is ever enforced, make it a kind-row attribute. — size: S

## Theme 3 — Tax exemption certificates (v5.4.2-09)

### exemption_type list repeated as a literal in a function
- where: v5.4.2-09:252-253 — function `tax_exemption_certificate_required()` (`NOT IN ('non_profit','government','agricultural','sales_for_resale','religious','educational','medical','other')`); mirrors the table CHECK narrowed by R-13 (tu.sql:15762-15763)
- rule: the exemption categories the Texas Comptroller model recognises; `industrial` removed because Texas predominant-use exemptions are per point of delivery — source: R-13, Comptroller Rule 3.287
- class: c
- other states: categories differ. Examples: direct-pay permits; manufacturing or predominant-use exemptions handled as rows elsewhere; tribal-member or on-reservation exemptions; federal government separately from state and local; states where residential service is taxable and a low-income exemption category exists (guesses on specific states). A second literal copy means any widening needs two DDL edits.
- fix: a platform table `tax_exemption_categories(state_code, service_type, category_code, effective_from/to, source_note)`. The table CHECK becomes an FK and the function reads the table. — size: M

### Certificate-required designation per tenant, not per jurisdiction
- where: v5.4.2-09:243-297 — function `tax_exemption_certificate_required()` reading `tenants.settings -> tax_exemptions -> certificate_required -> <type>`; hardcoded default TRUE at -09:285-286; precondition at -09:191
- rule: whether a category needs a certificate. The default TRUE is justified by "Comptroller Rule 3.287 puts certificate liability on the seller". The header itself says a flip is for "a jurisdiction that exempts government entities by law" (-09:42-45). — source: R-13, CI-046, Rule 3.287
- class: b
- other states: this is state tax law keyed by tenant, with no state_code, service type or effective dating. A multi-state tenant cannot have "certificate required in TX, exempt by status in state Y". A law change cannot be dated, and a flip applies to all history's future entries at once.
- fix: platform table `tax_exemption_certificate_rules(state_code, category, certificate_required, accepted_forms, effective_from/to, source_note)` with a lookup that raises when no row is in force (the meter_accuracy model). The tenant setting survives only as a stricter override. — size: M

## Theme 4 — Snapshot binding and corrections (v5.4.2-10 / -11)

### v1 snapshot contract is gas-only
- where: v5.4.2-10:743-753 and 769-781 — `validate_calculation_snapshot()` (required `gas_factors`: `pga_factor, btu_factor, pressure_factor, temperature_factor, meter_multiplier, factor_stack_intermediates`; `wna_inputs.applied`, plus HDD / deadband / heating_factor keys when applied)
- rule: every invoice's snapshot must carry gas-measurement and weather-normalization inputs — source: CI-015 (from v5.4.2-04, re-issued here)
- class: c
- other states / service types: a water or electric bill must fabricate null gas keys. Electric needs demand (kW), TOU periods and power factor. Water needs tier blocks and sewer winter-averaging. None has a required section. WNA is itself a per-state tariff mechanism, not universal.
- fix: service-type-specific snapshot sections, where the required-key contract is chosen by (schema_version, service_type), for example a `gas_factors` required only when the bill has gas lines. Or store the per-service contract as data. — size: M

### Snapshot tax_inputs hard-requires a franchise_fees section
- where: v5.4.2-10:749, 782-784 — `validate_calculation_snapshot()` (`tax_inputs` must have arrays `jurisdictions`, `exemptions`, `franchise_fees`)
- rule: the tax model is jurisdictions + certificate exemptions + municipal franchise fees (the Texas shape) — source: CI-015
- class: b
- other states: an empty array works, so this does not block. But utility-users taxes, gross-receipts pass-throughs and regulatory surcharges (the -07 rules) have no named slot, and -07's rule reads are not in the contract (a v2 is deferred, -07:132-134).
- fix: a generic `levies` array (type-tagged by jurisdiction data) in a v2 contract. — size: S

### Ordinary bill priced exactly at period_end
- where: v5.4.2-10:640-642 — `calculation_snapshot_coordinate_violation()`; stated at -10:37-41 and the COMMENT on `invoice_calculation_snapshots.valid_at` at -10:943-944
- rule: a non-correction bill's `valid_at` must equal `invoices.period_end`, so a bill spanning a rate change is priced wholly at end-of-period rates — source: v5.4.2-03 formula convention; Ryan 2026-09-08 "strict"
- class: c
- other states: which rates apply to a period that spans an effective date is a tariff or commission rule. Options include "service rendered on and after" with day-weighted proration, "bills rendered on and after", or end-of-period. Several commissions are believed to require proration across a rate change (a guess on specifics). A single coordinate pinned to period_end cannot express proration, or a bill-date convention.
- fix: a pricing-date convention as per-jurisdiction/tariff data (period_end | bill_date | prorate_by_days), with the binding admitting multiple valid_at segments when prorating. — size: L

### Correction rate-date default "historical" = "RRC-compliant"
- where: v5.4.2-10:953-954 — COMMENT on `billing_runs.correction_rate_mode` ("RRC-compliant default"); column DEFAULT `'historical'` + CHECK at tu.sql:2363-2364; fallback branches in `get_correction_rate_date()` at v5.4.2-11:653-664 ("'historical' is the default; covers NULL defensively", and the unrecognised-mode ELSE)
- rule: a corrected bill is re-priced at the voided bill's period rates unless the operator elects otherwise — source: CI-005, Kyle's correction-rate-mode ruling
- class: c (DEFAULT and function fallback constant)
- other states: another commission's rebilling rule may require the lower of old and new rates, or current rates for backbilled periods beyond some age. Those interact with backbilling caps (§7.45 analogues). The election modes are also a closed CHECK (`historical | current`).
- fix: the default mode from a per-jurisdiction rule row. The function's ELSE should raise rather than silently fall back to the Texas default. — size: S
