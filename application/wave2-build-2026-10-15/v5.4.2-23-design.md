# v5.4.2-23 design — cash: add-only money, reversals, payment application, credits from overpayment (2026-10-16)

**Authority:** Ryan, 2026-10-16: "split it, go with the default" (-23 = M, X, A, C; -24 = U, L). Rulings: R-02, R-05, R-06, K-05, K-07 (GBM `kyle-decisions-2026-10-15-…`), D7-1, D7-2, CI-050. Q1 (a) (Ryan, 2026-10-15).
**Spec:** `source-inventory-wave2.md`, sections M, X, A, C and "-23 triage".
**Migration cost:** meaning-changing. The cash tables are empty in every database we know of (nothing is live; the deploy check counts them), so each change is cheap now. A change made after go-live would mean rewriting every reader.

This is the shape the patch implements. A call marked **(rule)** is settled by a standing rule and reported to Ryan, not asked.

## X — reversals (R-02; CI-017)

- **X1. Which rows are reversals.** A ledger row is a reversal if and only if it names an entry (`reverses_ledger_entry_id`).
  - `void_reversal`, `nsf` and `write_off_recovery` must name one.
  - So must a `refund` row that returns a payment.
  - Every other type may name one, and then it is a reversal: a partial adjustment of a charge is an `adjustment` that names the charge.
  - -22's reversed recovery (an `adjustment` naming a `write_off_recovery`) stays valid.
- **X1b. Same customer (rule).** A reversal names an entry of its own tenant *and customer*. Aging and the close net reversals only within a customer (-22). An entry another customer's row names is money moved between customers, and that is a `transfer`, not a reversal.
- **X2. Sign and cap: -22's write-off rule, made general.** Every entry is the root of its tree (everything that names it, at any depth, within its customer).
  - A reversal has the opposite sign to the entry it names.
  - For every entry on the path from the new row up to its root, the net of that entry's tree (the entry itself excluded) lies between 0 and minus the entry's amount.
  - The cap holds at every date of the tree and at every recorded instant, exactly as -22's r4–r6 rules (date order; recorded order, 40001 for a row stamped at its transaction's start and 23514 for a chosen time; finite times).
  - A write-off is one case of the rule. Its recovery's own checks stay: it names a `write_off`, and only a recovery names a write-off directly.
- **X2 lock.** One advisory lock per tree, on the root's id, after the head. -22 took one lock per write-off above the row, in id order. One lock per tree means two writers under the same tree no longer deadlock with each other. Two transactions touching two trees in opposite orders still can, and writers retry 40P01. READ COMMITTED only, as in -22.
- **X3. The void reverses what is left.** `void_invoice()` posts minus (the charge entry plus its tree's net), not `-(amount_due)`. If nothing is left, it posts no ledger row and records a NULL reversal amount.
- **X4.** -21's "one void reversal per entry" stays: its unique index.

## M — add-only money (R-05; CI-013; Q1 (a))

- **M1. The counters go (rule: derive, do not store; the -19 `running_balance` precedent).** They are:
  - `payments.applied_amount` and `unapplied_amount`;
  - `customer_credits.applied_amount` and `remaining_amount`, with their CHECKs;
  - **`invoices.amount_paid` and `balance` (rule).** These are the same kind of counter. With applications as the record, a writable `amount_paid` would be a second, unchecked copy of the money (memory `two-fields-both-the-money`).
  - **Their reader goes too.** The `credit_aging_statistics` materialized view is rebuilt on the derived remaining amount, and the two indexes on `remaining_amount` and the one on `unapplied_amount` are dropped.
  - Read through `payment_position_as_of(payment, recorded)`, `customer_credit_position_as_of(credit, recorded)` and `invoice_line_position_as_of(line, recorded)` (memory `temporal-helper-no-live-fallback`: required coordinates; NULL raises).
- **M1b. `customer_credits.status` loses `fully_applied` (rule).** It is a counter in disguise. A credit with nothing left is read from the position, not stored. `active` means "not ended".
- **M2. Amounts and identity are write-once from insert.** This covers payments (today they are frozen only once they leave `pending`), customer credits (already frozen from insert; `regulatory_class` is not on them) and adhoc charges (today frozen once they leave `pending`; `regulatory_class` is added to the frozen list).
- **M3. Status moves forward only, and each move names the row behind it (Q1 (a)).**
  - A new column, `status_cause_id`, is written in the same UPDATE as the status and checked against the table that the target status names.
  - A payment:
    - `pending → posted` names its posting ledger entry (`payment`, or `deposit` for a deposit, referencing the payment, at minus its amount);
    - `→ nsf` names the `nsf` ledger row that reverses that posting entry;
    - `→ reversed` names a ledger reversal of that posting entry;
    - `→ refunded` names a refund payment that returns it;
    - `pending → voided` names nothing (no money moved; no ledger row references it).
  - A payment born `posted` is not a move.
  - **Guard the act:** a payment moves to `nsf`, `reversed` or `voided` only when its applications net to 0 and no live credit came from it. It moves to `refunded` only when nothing of it is left after the move. Bounced money cannot still be paying a bill.
  - A credit:
    - `→ refunded` names a refund payment from it, and nothing is left;
    - `→ voided` names the ledger reversal of its `credit_issued` entry, and nothing of it is applied;
    - `→ escheated` names its `escheatment_events` row;
    - `→ donated` names a `donation` ledger row referencing it, and nothing is left.
  - An adhoc charge's moves already name their row (`billed_on_invoice_id`, `voided_from_invoice_id`). `pending → void` or `→ waived` moves no money: the charge was never on the ledger. Its reason is required (the existing columns).
  - **One pointer, the latest move (rule).** The earlier move's row still names the payment or credit, so the history can be read back from the rows. This is not a second log (memory `app-written-logs-are-not-guards`).
- **M4. `payments.deposit_status` goes (rule).** Since -06 a deposit is a `deposits` row, and its status is projected onto `customers` from `deposit_events`. The payment's copy is a third, unprojected one. `is_deposit` stays: it is K-05's designation.

## A — payment application (R-06, K-05, D7-1; CI-049, CI-050)

- **A1. Per bill line.** `invoice_applications` gains `invoice_line_item_id`, NOT NULL. A composite FK (line, invoice, tenant) proves the line is on the bill.
- **A1b. Each line carries its class (triage call 1).** `invoice_line_items.regulatory_class`, NOT NULL, frozen with the bill. A line from a one-off charge matches that charge. A line from a rate item matches the rate item version in force on the bill's coordinates (`rate_item_version_as_of`).
- **A2. Add-only.** The `reversed_*` stamp and the writable `notes` go: the table is append-only. A mistaken application is undone by a row with a negative amount that names it (`undoes_application_id`), with a reason and a user. The undos against one application total at most its amount. An undo cannot itself be undone: to re-apply, write a new application.
- **A3. Caps, checked after the statement's rows are in (as -22's cap).**
  - A payment's money goes three ways: its applications, its overpayment credits (C1) and its refunds. Together they never exceed the payment.
  - A credit's money also goes three ways: its applications, its refunds, and its donation or escheat. Together they never exceed the credit.
  - A line's applications never exceed the line.
  - Only a positive line takes applications. A credit or adjustment line is the bill's own netting.
  - Only an issued, unvoided bill takes a new application. An undo is always allowed.
  - Only a posted, non-deposit payment and an active credit are a source.
  - **Locks:** an advisory lock on the source, then on the line, after the head. READ COMMITTED only.
- **A4. How each application was chosen.** `basis` is one of:
  - `oldest_first`;
  - `operator_override`, with a reason and the user (CI-049);
  - `restriction`;
  - `floor_over_restriction`;
  - `undo`.
  - An `oldest_first` or `restriction` row names the allocation tariff row in force (A5).
  - **Retired (rule: K-05 outranks the 2026-05 schema):** `tenants.payment_allocation_strategy` loses `newest_first`, `largest_first` and `manual_only`. Its one remaining value, `oldest_first`, makes the column dead, so it is dropped, with its history key.
- **A5. The within-bill order is a tariff row (triage call 2).** `payment_allocation_tariffs` is a `tariff` rule table under the rule-terms convention (kind `payment_allocation_tariff` v1).
  - The terms rank the bill line's `charge_type` values within each regulatory class.
  - The class order is a fixed term: regulated first. The schema admits no other.
  - Texas gets a `delegated_to_utility` law row (K-05: no statewide order; a city sets its own), in `payment_allocation_rules` (kind `payment_allocation` v1).
  - K-07: no default row; the tenant enters its own at setup.
- **A6. The restriction on the payment (D7-1 (b)).**
  - `payer_class` is `customer` (the default) or `agency`.
  - An agency payment may carry `restricted_to_invoice_id`, and optionally `restricted_to_line_item_id`, of the payment's own customer.
  - Write-once from insert.
  - An application from a restricted payment to its target says `restriction`.
  - An application elsewhere says `floor_over_restriction`, and it may land only on a regulated line while the target is unregulated (triage call 3: represented, not decided).
  - An unrestricted payment never says either.
  - -24's pledges point at these columns.

## C — credits from overpayment (R-06)

- **C1. `customer_credit_approvals`.** Append-only. It records the payment, the customer, the approved amount, the approving user (NOT NULL) and the time (the database's).
  - A credit of origin `overpayment` names its approval (`approval_id`, NOT NULL for that origin), and its amount equals the approved amount.
  - An approval is used by one credit at most (unique).
  - The approval's payment is the credit's `source_payment_id`.
- **C2.** `tenants.overpayment_handling` loses `apply_to_specific_invoices` (K-05: no designation of a bill). `hold_as_credit` and `refund_automatically` stay.

## The freeze and the seal

- Applications, approvals and the tariff rows are money or configuration dated by a day. An application is dated `applied_on`, a new NOT NULL date.
  - Application and approval writes take the head FOR SHARE, as every money write does (-22 P2).
  - An application dated on or before the closed-through date is refused.
- `assert_cash_guard_invariants()`: every -23 guard and constraint exists exactly as defined and ENABLE ALWAYS, the counters are absent, and the walks' plan settings are pinned. It runs in the tail and in CI.

## Lock order (stated as a rule, in -22's form)

A money write takes, in this order:
1. its tenant's head, FOR SHARE;
2. the advisory lock of the ledger tree it joins (a reversal) — or, for an application, the source's lock and then the line's lock;
3. nothing else.

The close's rule (-22) is unchanged: head, period, the closing user's row, the tenant row. A transaction that holds a later lock and waits, directly or through others, for an earlier one can deadlock. No list of shapes is promised complete; every writer retries 40P01. Post each act in a transaction of its own.

## Not in -23

- U (unmatched cash) and L (agency pledges): -24.
- A customer-initiation date: it exists already (-19: `payments.effective_date` and its method). Triage call 4 is corrected.
- Allocation itself (choosing lines, applying the floor, notifying about a restriction): the core.
- Per-line aging (-22 R3): the core reads the positions.
- Invoice status `paid` and `partial` stay app-written lifecycle (CI-012). A residual: they can disagree with the derived position.

## Corrections made in review (r2–r5)

This design is the r1 draft. Where review changed it, the patch header and these records govern (Codex r2 S3):
- **r2** (`v5.4.2-23-r2-plan.md`): a payment is born pending and posted by a move naming its posting entry, not born posted; only the reversal types, `adjustment` and `write_off` name an entry, not every type; an undo needs a source not yet ended; an approval and a status move take no head (they move no money).
- **r3** (`v5.4.2-23-r3-plan.md`; DECISION-LOG 2026-10-21):
  - Kyle's K-10: a refund is its own movement of money and names no entry (X1); a pending refund blocks the bounce; every draw is capped by the source less what was taken back under its arrival entry, at every date.
  - Ryan: payments and credits have no `refunded` status, credits no `donated` (M3's lists); both are read from the positions.
  - One arrival entry per source; positions read what stands on the ledger; remittances are frozen and on their credit's own account.
- **r4** (`v5.4.2-23-r4-plan.md`; DECISION-LOG 2026-10-21): K-10 through the overpayment credit (one live credit per payment); arrival rows only while their source is pending or active; nothing given back to an ended source; a returned refund of an escheated credit goes into a `refund_reissue` credit (Ryan, option 1).
- **r5** (`v5.4.2-23-r5-plan.md`; DECISION-LOG 2026-10-28): one split of a take-back between a payment and its overpayment credit (`overpayment_split`), from what each still held, and the two capped together (K-10's refund cap); nothing under a refund's posting row once it came back; the reissue carries what is left, at most the refund; no future status time; arrival rows at READ COMMITTED.
