## 1. Sources are read under their lock (Opus B1, Fable B2, Codex B2; executed by all three)

- `check_cash_source(kind, id, row_created_at, purpose, on_date)` takes the source lock **first**, then reads the source and decides eligibility itself:
  - `application`: a posted non-deposit payment, or an active credit;
  - `overpayment_credit`: a posted non-deposit payment;
  - `refund`: a posted payment, or an active credit;
  - `undo`: the source is posted / active (Opus B3: an undo on a refunded, donated or escheated source would bring money back to an ended source);
  - dated on or after the source; the source's money on the ledger is not reversed (section 3).
  The two triggers keep only the checks on the row itself. [guard the act; count-guards need a lock handshake]
- **Unknown or invisible source fails closed** (`NOT FOUND` raises). [Fable S5]
- **A status move that ends a source runs at READ COMMITTED only** (25000), as every draw does: under REPEATABLE READ the move's snapshot hides an application committed while it waited (Codex B2 schedule 2). [count-guards need a lock handshake]

## 2. Status moves name the act, exactly (Codex B4, Opus B2 O3, Fable S3, S4)

- **Births.** A payment is born `pending` (default changes from `posted`); a credit is born `active`. A payment born in any other status is refused. `posted` is then always a move naming its posting entry. [Q1 (a); guard the act]
- `posted` names a ledger row of type `payment` (a bill payment), `deposit` (a deposit) or `refund` (a refund payment), referencing the payment, at minus its amount.
- `nsf` names an `nsf` row reversing the posting entry, **and the posting entry is then fully reversed** (remaining 0). `reversed` the same, with any reversal type but `nsf`. A 1.00 row no longer ends a 30.00 payment. [guard the act]
- `refunded` names a refund **pending or posted**. [Fable S4]
- A credit's `voided`:
  - a credit of any origin but `overpayment` names the reversal of its `credit_issued` entry, and that entry is then fully reversed;
  - an `overpayment` credit posts no ledger row (its money is the payment's, already on the ledger; a `credit_issued` row would count it twice), so its void names nothing, like a pending payment's. Correction to the -23 design's M3 text. [two fields, both the money]
- `escheated` names an `escheatment_events` row of type `escheated_to_state` whose `amount_related` is what is left; `donated` a `donation` ledger row whose amount is what is left. Both are draws (section 4).

## 3. Money on the ledger (Opus B2 O1, O2, O6)

- **A source draws only while its arrival stands.** A payment: its posting entry (its `status_cause_id` while posted) is not reversed, except by its own refunds (K1). A credit of any origin but `overpayment`: its `credit_issued` entry is not reversed. Checked in `check_cash_source` under the lock.
- **The bounce row is guarded where it is written.** A ledger row reversing a payment's posting entry (other than its refund's) is refused while the payment is applied or a live overpayment credit draws on it; a row reversing a credit's `credit_issued` entry is refused while the credit is applied or refunded. It takes the source lock after its tree's (tree, then source; nothing takes them the other way). [guard the act, not the label; guards must act]

## 4. Every way money leaves a source is a draw (Fable B1, S1; Codex B1)

- **A credit names a payment only if it is an overpayment credit** (CHECK: `source_payment_id IS NULL OR origin_type = 'overpayment'`). The draw, the trigger's WHEN and the approval key on `source_payment_id` (the act). Deposit refund credits name `deposit_id`, not a payment, so -15 is unchanged. [guard the act, not the label]
- **A voided overpayment credit stops drawing** on its payment, from the instant it is voided (section 5).
- **A credit's donation and escheat are draws:** a `donation` ledger row referencing a credit, and an `escheated_to_state` event, take the credit's lock and count in `cash_source_draws` and the position. A refund after a full donation is refused by the cap; a donation of 1.00 cannot end a credit with 100 left.

## 5. Positions are reproducible at their coordinates (Codex B3, Opus B3, Fable N2)

- **R2 is closed, not carried.** `payments.status_recorded_at` and `customer_credits.status_recorded_at`, stamped by the database on every status move (write-once per move, never caller-set; a superuser's history load keeps its own). Moves are forward only and every counted status (`pending`, `posted`; `active`) comes before every uncounted one, so a refund (or credit) counts at a recorded instant R exactly when it was recorded by R and, if its status is now uncounted, it left after R.
- **A line has no position before its bill was issued.** The line and bill readers return no row unless the bill's `invoice_date` is on or before the money date and its `first_issued_at` (database-stamped, -10) on or before the recorded instant. A draft bill's lines have no position.
- **The recorded-instant caveat** (Opus S1, Fable N2) is -22's model, stated in each reader's comment: a position at (d, R) is fixed once R is older than every open transaction. [design permanent: say it where it is read]

## 6. Smaller fixes

- **Recorded order covers the source and the line themselves** (Opus S3): a draw recorded before its source, or an application before its bill was issued, is refused (23514).
- **No reversal dated after today** (Opus S2, Fable N11): a row naming an entry is dated on or before `CURRENT_DATE`, so a future row can't block a void.
- **A payment row cannot "reverse" a charge** (Fable S7): the only types that may name an entry are the reversal types (`void_reversal`, `nsf`, `write_off_recovery`, `refund`), `adjustment`, and `write_off` (-22's nested write-off: part of a recovery that came back is written off under it; -22 battery R12n). Every other type is new money or a move of money (a payment, a charge, a transfer, a donation of a credit), not an undo, and names nothing; a donation or escheat points at its credit through `reference_id` and is capped as a draw (section 4), not as a reversal. [guard the act, not the label: R-02 is about undoing]
- **An approval is signed by the tenant's own user** (Opus S4): FK `(approved_by, tenant_id)` to `users (id, tenant_id)`.
- **The invariants check column grants** (all three): `has_any_column_privilege` for UPDATE/INSERT/DELETE reach. They also pin `payments.status`'s default, the new tables' RLS policies and `check_cash_source`'s EXECUTE grants (Fable N9). tally_core loses the surplus `ledger_tree_lock_key` EXECUTE (Fable N5).
- **The lock order is restated:** the void's own invoice row (FOR UPDATE) comes before the head; a status move's own row lock comes before its source lock; a guarded ledger reversal takes its tree, then its source (Codex N4, Fable N3). The approval and a status move take no head: they move no money, and the rows that do (ledger, applications) take it. This corrects the design's "approval writes take the head" (Codex S3).
