## K. Kyle's answer, applied

### K1 = A: a refund is not a reversal

- **X1 loses `refund`.** A `refund` ledger row names no entry: drop it from `account_ledger_reversal_types_name_check` (types that must name an entry) and from `account_ledger_reversal_types_only_check` (types that may). A refund row points at its refund through `reference_id`, as a donation points at its credit. [K1 = A]
- **The refund exemption is deleted, not fixed:**
  - `enforce_ledger_reversal`'s bounce-row guard: no "unless it is one of the payment's own refunds" leg;
  - `cash_arrival_stands`: the posting entry's tree has no refund rows to skip.
  This deletes round 2's Opus B2, Codex B2 and Fable B1.
- **A credit's refund (an overpayment credit's included) posts as its own row** under the refund, naming nothing. That deletes Opus B5, Codex B4 and Fable B2. A test must post one.
- **The invariants:** the two X1 CHECK strings in `assert_cash_guard_invariants()` change; mutation N77 changes with them (Fable).

### Kyle's two caps

- **Bounce or reversal ≤ payment − earlier reversals.** This is R-02's tree cap once refunds are out of X1: the $50 bounce after a $30 refund goes through. No new code; a test pins the example (payment 50, refund 30 posted, bounce 50, customer owes 30), with the balance read by `account_balance_as_of()`.
- **Refund ≤ payment − earlier refunds − earlier reversals.** r2 refuses *any* draw once the posting entry has *any* reversal under it (`cash_arrival_stands` is a yes/no). That is stricter than Kyle's cap for a partial reversal: an `adjustment` reversing 5 of a 50 posting entry should leave 45 to draw on, not 0. r3 makes the money that stands an amount:
  - `cash_arrival_stands` becomes `cash_arrival_left(kind, id, on_date)`: the posting entry (or `credit_issued` entry) netted with everything under it, at the date;
  - `check_cash_source` caps the draws (applications, overpayment credits, refunds; a credit's donations and escheats) at that amount, at every date, not at the source's face amount;
  - a full bounce leaves 0, so a refund after a bounce is refused (Kyle: "we never pay out money we no longer hold").
  The same cap binds applications and the other draws, since they are money leaving the same source. [Kyle's cap; guard the act, not the label]
- **Boundary tests at concrete values on both sides** [test the boundary]: posting 50, adjustment −5 under it: a refund of 45 admitted, 45.01 refused; after a full nsf, a refund of 0.01 refused.

### K2: a pending refund blocks a bounce

- A payment's move to `nsf` or `reversed`, and a ledger row reversing its posting entry (the row or the move), are refused while a refund of it is `pending`. The operator cancels the refund (`pending` → `voided`) first, and the record shows it. No automatic cancel (Kyle: the refund may already be in a check run or ACH batch). All three round-2 reviewers found that no code does this. -19's P5c fixture relies on the admission and changes. [K2; guard the act]
- **UI, not schema:** the bounce screen shows the pending refund with a one-click cancel. A note for Kyle's ui-concepts, not in this patch.

### K3: return-window holds

- Nothing in the database. A tenant-configurable hold period or warning, off by default, read by the app or core. Where the setting is stored is additive later (a tenant configuration row), so no column now. [schema represents, core evaluates; frame shapes by migration cost: additive]

## Ryan's call during r3 (2026-10-21): refunded and donated are not statuses

Kyle's answer left one gap that the round-2 reviewers hit from different sides (Opus B4, Fable S3, Codex B5 and B7): a payment marked refunded, or a credit marked donated, was final, so a refund check that bounced or a donation keyed in error had no record. Ryan chose option 1: stop storing them (DECISION-LOG 2026-10-21; memory `refunded-donated-derived`).
- Payments: pending, posted, nsf, reversed, voided. Credits: active, escheated, voided.
- A refund that leaves (voided, nsf, reversed) and an overpayment credit's void take their source's lock; `check_cash_source` retries a draw begun before such a move.
- This replaces R6, R9 and the K2-adjacent refund guards: a failed refund (R8e–R8h) and an undone donation (S9) simply free the money; an undo on an ended source is still refused (R12j, an escheated credit).
