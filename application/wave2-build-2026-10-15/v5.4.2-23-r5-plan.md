## Blocking

### B1. A refund that came back can be paid out again (Opus B2, Fable S1, Codex B1; executed)
Nothing guards rows under a refund's own posting row. After the refund's return, re-reversing the return re-pays it: 80 out of a 50 payment, or the same 30 out twice beside its reissue.
**Fix:** a row under a refund's posting row takes the refund's lock; once the refund is nsf or reversed, nothing more goes under it. [guard the act]

### B2. A payment and its overpayment credit read a take-back differently (Opus B1, Fable B1, Codex S1; executed)
The payment's cap counts the credit at its full amount and the take-back on top; the credit counts it too; a take-back recorded before the credit is subtracted from it again. Money is stranded, or one side reads a surplus the other's cap refuses.
**Fix:** one rule read by both positions and both caps. A take-back recorded after the credit comes out of the credit first, up to its amount (`absorbed`); the rest, and any take-back before the credit, is the payment's. Payment: credited = credit − absorbed; its cap counts the take-back less absorbed. Credit: issued = credit − absorbed; its cap counts absorbed. Their remainders then add up to the ledger. [K-10: what was paid out stands; test the boundary]

### B3. A give-back under a payment restores money to its escheated overpayment credit (Fable B2, Codex B2; executed)
**Fix:** a row giving money back under a payment's posting entry is refused while the payment's overpayment credit is escheated. [Ryan, 2026-10-21: nothing comes back to an escheated credit]

### B4. A refund that comes back after its credit's payment also bounced cannot be settled (Codex B3; executed)
Overpayment credit 100 escheated (refund 30 out, 70 remitted), its payment taken back in full, then the refund returns: no reissue fits the cap, and the commit check refuses. With a partial take-back the check passes with no reissue.
**Fix:** what came back is carried only as far as it leaves the escheated credit with money: a reissue carries exactly the credit's positive remainder (at most the refund's amount), and the commit check requires nothing *positive* left. Where the payment also bounced, the returned refund offsets money that never arrived: nothing is carried, and the customer owes the rest (K-10). *Refines Ryan's option 1 ("exactly what came back") for the case where the payment also bounced; reported, not asked.* [K-10; Ryan, 2026-10-21]

### B5. A superuser's future status time hides the commit check (Codex B4; executed)
**Fix:** `status_recorded_at` is never in the future or non-finite, for any role; a history load chooses only past times. [every row is recorded at a time already reached (-22)]

### B6. A posting or credit_issued row at REPEATABLE READ reads a stale status (Fable B3; executed, two sessions)
**Fix:** the READ COMMITTED pin runs before the arrival-row checks. [count-guards need a lock handshake]
