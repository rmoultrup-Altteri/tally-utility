## Blocking

### B1. A take-back is not ordered against the draws it is split against (Opus B2, Fable B1; both executed, two sessions)
A row under a payment's posting entry, from a transaction that began before a refund of the credit (or before the credit's creation) but writes after it, is read at the earlier instant. The split then has the credit absorbing money it had already paid out, or misses a credit of 60. Every draw is ordered against the tree; nothing orders the tree row against the draws. A history-load variant is admitted too, where it should be refused with 23514.
**Fix:** check_cash_source's three recorded-order legs, applied to rows under a payment's posting entry. Such a row is not recorded before a draw on the payment or its live credit, before the credit's creation, or before a draw that has since left. 40001 for a row stamped at its transaction's start, 23514 for a chosen time. Add a race and a mutation. [recorded order, -22]

### B2. The shortfall is placed at the take-back, so one side can show money while the other shows a deficit (Opus B1, Codex B2; both executed)
When the payment's own refund comes back, or a give-back follows, the payment and the credit read, for example, 40 and −10 while only 30 can be drawn. They then stay at 10 and −10, which is round 4's P2 end state.
**Fix:** decide which side carries a shortfall when a position is read, not when the take-back is recorded. If one side reads negative while the other reads positive, the positive side covers the deficit as far as it can.
- The caps already admit exactly this (the cap on the two together; Opus B1, Fable N6).
- The escheat check, the reissue's remainder and the commit check read the credit's covered position.
- Test: one helper that checks "each remainder equals what its cap admits, and the two add up to the ledger" over every W pair (Opus, Fable S3).

[K-10; a position equals what its cap admits]

### B3. Corrections written in one transaction are split in an order set by their ids (Codex B3; executed)
A take-back and its give-back recorded in one transaction are processed in uuid order. A give-back that comes first absorbs nothing, and the credit loses 20 for good.
**Fix:** within one instant, order a row before the rows under it (tree depth, then id). Test both id orders and distinct dates. [the tree's own order]

### B4. A past status-time correction rewrites the split (Codex B4; executed)
An owner moves a returned refund's `status_recorded_at` to a later past time, with the status unchanged. That needs no locks and no checks, and it puts +10 back on an escheated credit.
**Fix:** `status_recorded_at` changes only with its status, for every role. A history load's chosen time is not before a row already recorded on that source's lineage (23514). [write-once; recorded order]

### B5. A reissue made before the payment bounces escapes the payment's cap (Codex B1; executed)
Payment 100, overpayment credit 100, a refund out and back, then a reissue. After the payment bounces in full, the reissue still pays out its 30, or 100 if its credit was still active.
**Fix:** a reissue descended from an overpayment credit stays in its payment's lineage:
- its draws take the payment's lock and are capped by the cap on the two together, which counts the reissue's draws, not its amount;
- the bounce and the move to nsf wait while it is applied or has a refund pending;
- in the split, its unspent money goes with the payment, as the credit's does.

What it paid out stands. [K-10, through the reissue as through the credit]
