## Blocking

### B1. The cover nets the payment against the family's total, never one credit against another (Fable B1, Opus B2, Codex B1; all three executed)
A shortfall lands on the overpayment credit K. A reissue R can then regain money, through its own refund coming back or a give-back that restores its share first. R reads money the pool refuses, while K carries a deficit that is partly not owed. Fable P1: K −50, R +20, agree() fails, and a refund of 0.01 from R is refused (130.01 > 100). K can then be neither voided nor escheated. Codex 01 reproduces it through a give-back; Opus through a refund's return on a history load.
**Fix:** in `overpayment_absorbed_as_of`, once each member's own figure is built, cover a member in deficit first from the other members holding money (the latest reissue first, as a give-back restores the latest share first), then from the payment. The caps stay as they are: R's own cap admits 20, and the pool refuses it.
- Test: Fable P1 and P1c (the payment holds 40 of its own), Opus F, Codex 01, all through agree().
- Add a mutation for the new leg (Fable N7: N194/N195 drop only the two directions between the payment and the family).

[a position equals what its cap admits; credit-first attribution, R20]

### B2. An escheat admitted on the covered position leaves money on the escheated credit later (Opus B1; executed as tally_app, each act its own transaction, and as a history load)
The escheat check reads the credit's covered position. A credit whose own side holds money while it covers the payment's deficit reads 0, and is escheated. When the payment's own refund later comes back, the payment reads 30 and the escheated credit +10. The utility holds 40 of the customer's money and can refund only 30. The bank's give-back of the payment's own 10 is now refused by the escheated-credit guard. agree() passes, because it skips escheated credits.
**Fix:** a credit is escheated only when its own side, without the cover, holds nothing positive either. agree() checks escheated credits too.

[Ryan, 2026-10-21: nothing comes back to an escheated credit; a position equals what its cap admits]

### B3. A voided reissue keeps its share in the payment's cap but drops out of the positions (Opus B3; executed, history load)
`overpayment_split`, which the payment's cap reads, keeps the voided reissue's share. `overpayment_absorbed_as_of` skips voided members. The void also returns the reissue's whole amount to the credit it came from, not what the reissue still held. Opus's shape: the payment reads 40 but admits a refund of 50; the pool holds the total at 70, so no money escapes.
**Fix:** a reissue's void passes its share to the credit it came from, with what it still held, not its amount. The reissue then holds nothing in the split, and its predecessor reads what the void returned.
- Test: Opus V through agree().
- Add a mutation.

[the reissue in its payment's lineage, r6 B5; a position equals what its cap admits]

### B4. The 1,000th reissue loses its payment (Codex B2; outcome: see the probe outputs)
`overpayment_root` starts at depth 1 and `overpayment_family` at depth 0; both stop at 1000 without saying so. At generation 1000 the root comes back NULL, which every reader treats as "not descended from an overpayment". The reissue then takes no payment lock and skips the pool. Codex predicts a payout admitted after the payment has come back in full. This is a credit-lineage limit, separate from R1's bound on ledger reversal trees.
**Fix:** a walk that reaches its bound raises; it never returns NULL. A reissue past the bound is refused when it is made. Both walks use one bound, checked by an invariant.

## As built (2026-10-29, in progress)

Every blocking and should-fix item is built. Three points differ from the plan or go beyond it:

1. **B3: the void is refused, not the share passed on.** A reissue carrying a share of a take-back is not voided; once the share is given back, the void is admitted (Z5a, Z5b). Passing the share to the credit the reissue came from would need the shares to move at the void's instant, row by row of the take-back, for every reader and cap, which is a new path through all of them. Refusing strands nothing: what the reissue holds stays drawable on it (refunded or applied). [the reissue in its payment's lineage; a position equals what its cap admits]
2. **B2: a credit whose money covers a deficit beside it stays active, reading nothing,** until the deficit goes (Z4b: the payment's refund comes back, and the credit reads its 10 again). [Ryan, 2026-10-21: nothing comes back to an escheated credit]
3. **The shares are read once per reading** in `overpayment_absorbed_as_of`, not once per family credit. Codex's 1,000-reissue probe ran over 50 minutes on r6 without finishing, because every act read the whole family once per member.

**B4** is one bound, `reissue_line_bound()` (999), read by `reissue_generation`, `overpayment_root`, `overpayment_family` and the reissue's make. Each walk raises 22000 on a deeper line.
