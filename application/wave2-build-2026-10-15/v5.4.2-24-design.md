## 1. What is and isn't the gap (rule: WF-U)

- **An identified customer whose money has no target** (overpaid, no open bill, the operator unsure which bill) already has its substrate. Since -23, a payment's remainder is derived (`payment_position_as_of`) and is applied, held as an approved credit, or refunded (R-06, R16). `manual_only` allocation is retired (-23 A4), so WF-U's "two populations look alike" no longer arises: a remainder is always an exception. -24 adds nothing here.
- **The unidentified payer** has no row at all. The lockbox file's control total cannot reconcile, and nothing can be aged or reported. This is what -24 builds: the receipt, held apart from any customer until it becomes a payment.
- **A multiple-candidate match** is an unidentified receipt with candidates recorded. A guess is never a payment (CI-053).

## 2. Unmatched receipts (U1–U3)

### 2.1 The receipt

`suspense_receipts`, add-only, one row per item of money the utility holds but cannot attribute:

- tenant; amount (> 0); `received_on` (the money date; frozen by the period close, -22 P2);
- `created_at`, stamped by the database (rule: the lesson `app-written-logs-are-not-guards`): when the utility recorded the receipt, the knowledge coordinate of every reader. **The service-level clock runs from `received_on`, NOT NULL** (rule: CI-056's NULL-clock failure; WF-U, "the import run is not the receipt"). The utility has held the money since the bank's date, and a receipt migrated from a legacy system keeps its true age.
- channel and source system (the `payments` vocabularies), and the instrument evidence: reference number, check number, check date, bank name, the provider's transaction id. These are the same columns as a payment, so that a resolution can carry them over unchanged.
- the payer as the item shows it: a name, an address, an account number as written (free text, nullable). A remitting agency, if the item shows one (`agency_id`, section 4.1).
- evidence references: the intake file and row (`import_staging`, where it came by file), an image or document reference. At least one evidence field must have content (lesson `blank-checks-whitelist-alnum`).
- `recorded_by`.

The intake channel's idempotency key is the provider's transaction id, unique per tenant and source system across both `payments` and `suspense_receipts`. A receipt cannot be recorded twice, nor once as a receipt and once as a payment (CI-055; the -23 unique index extends by a trigger check across the two tables).

### 2.2 Candidates

`suspense_receipt_candidates`, add-only: a customer the intake or an operator thought the money might belong to, with a basis (`intake_match`, `operator`), a confidence where intake computed one, and a note. A candidate is evidence, never an application (rule: CI-053). A candidate row does not move the money or change the receipt.

### 2.3 How a receipt leaves suspense

The money leaves by one of four acts, each a recorded row. **What a receipt still holds is derived, never stored (rule: -23 M1):** amount, less what went to payments, less what was disposed of, plus what came back.

1. **It becomes a payment (rule: WF-U, U2).** A `payments` row of the identified customer names the receipt (`suspense_receipt_id`, a two-column key with the tenant). Its instrument evidence is copied from the receipt, and the database checks the copy. The provider's transaction id stays on the receipt alone: it identifies the receipt once (CI-055), and two payments split from one receipt cannot both carry it. A resolution needs a recorded reason and user (rule: CI-049, CI-053's "resolution reasoning"): `payments` gains `suspense_resolution_reason` and `suspense_resolved_by`, required together with the receipt, write-once. One receipt may become several payments (a household paying two accounts with one check). **Dates (rule: -22 P2 and CI-058).** The payment is dated when it is posted, on or after the receipt's `received_on`. Money identified after its month was closed can then still be posted, in the open period. Its effective date, which decides timeliness and late fees, is no later than `received_on`: the customer had paid by the day the money came, and a postmark may be earlier. The rule binds when the effective date is written, at insert or at posting.
2. **It goes back to whoever sent it (rule: CI-053).** A disposition row of kind `returned_to_sender`, with the outgoing instrument (check number, provider id) and its date.
3. **The bank takes it back (rule: -23's NSF).** A disposition of kind `bank_returned`, with the return code, for money that bounces while still in suspense. Whatever already became a payment bounces through -23 as that payment's NSF, not through the receipt.
4. **It is reported as unclaimed property (rule: CI-053's "escheatment routing per jurisdiction"; Texas Property Code ch. 74 requires a holder to report property whose owner is unknown).** A disposition of kind `escheated`, with the jurisdiction and the report reference. When it is due is the core's, reading the state's dormancy rule. `escheatment_events` stays keyed to credits: a receipt has no customer, so its escheat is its own disposition, not a credit event.

`suspense_receipt_dispositions` is add-only. A mistaken disposition is undone by a row of kind `undo` naming it, at most its amount, and an undo is never undone (rule: -23 A2). A returned check that comes back uncashed is an undo of its `returned_to_sender`.

**The cap (rule: -23 A3).** What went to payments plus what was disposed of never exceeds the receipt, at every date. A payment that names a receipt counts against it unless it is `voided` (it never posted). A payment `reversed` as posted to the wrong customer gives its share back to the receipt, so the operator can post it to the right one. A payment that went `nsf` keeps its share counted, because the bank took that money back. The writers take the row-mutex handshake on the receipt (lesson `count-guards-need-lock-handshake`), in -23's order: tenant head, then receipt, then payment.

**No guessed application (rule: CI-053).** Nothing in the database chooses a customer. A payment naming a receipt is the operator's act, with the reason. The candidates table is not consulted by any guard.

### 2.4 Retention and the service level (U3, CI-056)

- **Retention (rule: CI-053 "configured per tenant"; U3).** `tenants.settings.suspense_retention_days` (a whole number > 0, no default: K-07, no assumed defaults; written to `tenant_configuration_history` like the other tenant settings, with an as-of reader as -22's aging edges have). After it, the receipt is to be returned or reported. When the period runs out, the core flags the receipt and the operator acts. The database does not dispose of money on a clock (memory `schema-represents-core-evaluates`).
- **The service level (rule: CI-056; R-11's direction).** `tenants.settings.suspense_service_level_days` (a whole number > 0, no default; history). A receipt still holding money past it is `aged_suspense` for -26's decisions. Which acts it bars, and where, is wave 7's rule data.
- **Settings keys, not columns (rule: additive).** `tenant_configuration_history` already records any `settings.*` key. A new column would extend that table's known-key CHECK, which -22's and -23's invariants list exactly. A CHECK on `tenants` holds each value to a whole number above 0.
- **Unset is allowed, and fails loud (rule: CI-053's "not silently dropped" outranks a required setting).** Both columns are nullable. A NOT NULL column with no default would refuse every tenant created without it. A receipt refused because the setting is missing would be money with no record, the very gap -24 closes. So a receipt is always recorded, and the readers that need a setting raise while it is unset at the instant read. To -26 that is an unevaluable condition, which blocks the act (fail toward protection).
- **The reader.** `suspense_receipt_position_as_of(receipt, on, recorded)`: amount, to payments, disposed, still held, and age from `received_on`. `suspense_receipts_aged_as_of(tenant, on)` lists every receipt holding money past the service level, with its candidates. A NULL coordinate raises (lesson `temporal-helper-no-live-fallback`). Both are what the core reads for -26's `aged_suspense`, and for the fail-loud surface.
- **Which accounts the bar touches (rule: CI-056; WF-U's PROMISe case).** An aged receipt bars a disconnection on each of its candidate customers. That is the one link the database supplies, the candidate rows. Whether the bar applies is the core's, reading -26 and the rule.

### 2.5 Where it sits in the books (rule: D-2, the subledger)

A receipt is not on any customer's ledger: it is the utility's liability to an unknown owner. There is no `account_ledger` row until it becomes a payment, and then the payment posts as any payment does. The period close's snapshot (-22 S1) should gain one line per tenant for money held in suspense at the close instant, so the subledger's total reconciles to the bank (known limit L4: the reader now, the line later). The receipt's and dispositions' dates join the close's freeze list and `assert_periods_guard_invariants()`.

## 3. Who the agencies are (rule: WF-L "a key, not a string")

`assistance_agencies`: per tenant, an agency the utility takes pledges and payments from. It has a name, the program it runs (`liheap_ceap`, `state_crisis`, `charity`, `government_other`) and contact details. It is retired by a stamped status, never deleted (lesson `correct-by-status-not-delete`).

`assistance_agency_agreements`, dated and recorded (the rule-terms template's shape): the agreement in force with that agency. It holds the pledge payment window in days (VA-26: 45), the eligibility hold in business days (VA-26: 5), and the agreement's source reference (the signed vendor agreement, its revision). **These are contract terms, not law (rule: memory `rule-terms-convention-adopted`; a charity's terms differ from CEAP's).** The protection a pledge gives under statute (TAC-7.460, a billing period) is law-row data for wave 7, read by the core beside the agreement.

## 4. Agency pledges (L1–L2)

### 4.1 The pledge

`agency_pledges`, add-only:

- tenant, customer, agency, the agreement in force when the pledge was received (a two-column key);
- `amount` (> 0, typed: WF-L);
- `pledged_on` (the agency's date) and `received_at` (stamped by the database: the clock, CI-056). The vendor agreement's window runs from the pledge; the service level runs from the utility's receipt. Both are kept.
- `pay_by`: the date the agency must pay by. The database computes it at insert, from `pledged_on` and the agreement's window, and it is write-once (memory `design-permanent-record-on-change`: stored when decided, not recomputed by readers). The agreement is the database's choice too: the agency's agreement in force on `pledged_on`.
- **A pledge with no agreement on file is still recorded (rule: fail toward protection).** Refusing it would leave the customer with no record of the pledge and so no protection. Its agreement and `pay_by` stay NULL, the reader's state is `no_agreement`, and -26 reads that as unevaluable, which blocks the act.
- the form it came in (`written`, `verbal`, `portal`) and its reference. 16 TAC §7.460(b)(2) speaks of a written pledge or letter of intent; whether a verbal pledge counts is the law row's term, and the record carries the form.
- the bills or charges it is toward (optional; the agency payment's restriction carries what it pays, D7-1).
- `recorded_by`.

**Several pledges coexist (rule: WF-L; CI-064's additive composition).** No uniqueness on customer and agency: LIHEAP toward the arrears and a charity toward the current bill are two rows.

### 4.2 How a pledge ends (rule: Q1 (a), forward-only status naming its row)

A pledge is `open`, then ends exactly once, by a recorded row in `agency_pledge_events` (add-only):

- **paid**: by one or more `payments` of `payer_class = 'agency'`, of the pledge's agency, that name the pledge (`agency_pledge_id`, two-column key). What they bring counts while posted: a pending payment has not cleared, and a bounced, reversed or voided one brought nothing. When it reaches the pledge, the pledge is paid in full. A payment short of it leaves the pledge open until `pay_by` (VA-26: "the gap is the customer's"). **No cap (rule: CI-053, money is never refused).** An agency that sends more than it pledged has still sent the money. The payment is taken, the pledge reads paid, and the excess is the agency payment's own money under -23's rules (and section 4.4 if refunded).
- **lapsed**: `pay_by` passed with the pledge not paid in full. The event records what was paid. This is WF-L's "a pledge made and not honoured", kept for the agency's history. The core records it, because the database does not act on a clock. A pledge past `pay_by` with no event reads as `overdue` in the reader (fail loud, CI-056).
- **withdrawn**: the agency cancels it, with a reason and the agency's reference.
- **recorded in error**: the operator's correction, with a reason. The pledge then never counted.

A payment naming a pledge after it has lapsed or been withdrawn is still taken (the money is real). It names the pledge, and the event history shows the late payment. A payment never names a pledge recorded in error.

`agency_pledge_position_as_of(pledge, on, recorded)`: amount, paid, outstanding, `pay_by`, and a state of `open`, `overdue`, `paid`, `lapsed`, `withdrawn` or `void`. `agency_pledges_open_as_of(tenant, on)` serves the fail-loud surface. The core reads these for -26's `agency_pledge` protection. How much of the balance it shelters, and for how long, is the rule's (VA-26's window, TAC-7.460's billing period, a city tariff).

### 4.3 Agency payments name their agency (rule: WF-L; -23 A6)

`payments.agency_id` (a two-column key) is required when `payer_class = 'agency'` and refused otherwise. A payment naming a pledge must be of the pledge's agency and customer. The column is write-once from insert, like `payer_class`.

### 4.4 Where an agency's leftover goes (rule: TAC-6.312, VA-26, CI-053's "refund to the originating party")

An agency payment's money may pay the customer's bills, and its leftover may be held as a credit (-23 R-06) for later bills. **A refund of it goes to the agency, never to the customer.** That covers a refund of the payment's remainder, of a credit whose origin is that payment, and of a reissue descended from one. 10 TAC §6.312(f) says this for CEAP money, VA-26 says it for an agency-paid deposit, and CI-053 says money goes back to whoever sent it. A refund row (`payment_method = 'refund'`) whose money traces to an agency payment must carry that agency in `agency_id`, and one whose money traces to the customer must carry none. The trace follows -23's existing lineage (`refunds_payment_id`, `refunds_customer_credit_id`, the credit's origin payment). The new column says, on a refund, to whom the refund was paid. `payer_class` keeps its -23 meaning on money in. A refund's `payer_class` is left as -23 writes it, and the comment on `agency_id` states the reading.

An agency-paid deposit's return (VA-26) should go the same way. -15 returns a deposit through its deposit events and a ledger entry, not through a refund payment that names a payee, so the check needs a payee on -15's return. That is a deposits change, and it is known limit L5.

### 4.5 The program type retires (rule: -25's precedent for `pending_dispute`)

`agency_pledge` in `program_types` takes no new enrollment, and its description says a pledge is an `agency_pledges` row. Apply refuses if any enrollment of that type exists (none does on `tally`), so nothing silently loses its meaning (DEPLOY-VERIFICATION -24). The type was never protective (CI-061's grade), so `do_not_disconnect` is unchanged.

## 5. The rest, by the cash patches' rules

- **Tenant keys and RLS (rule: R-12).** Every key between the new tables, and from `payments` to them, is a two-column key with `tenant_id`. Every new table has RLS and FORCE RLS with the tenant policy, and is never deleted (`no_hard_delete`, `no_truncate`).
- **Freeze (rule: -22 P2).** `received_on`, the disposition dates and the pledge's money dates are frozen by the close.
- **Locks (rule: -23's order, `lock-order-as-a-rule`).** As built (section 8): the tenant head, then the payment's own -23 locks, then the receipt or pledge. Every writer retries 40P01 and 40001. READ COMMITTED only.
- **Invariants.** `assert_cash_guard_invariants()` gains the new tables' rules. A new `assert_suspense_pledge_guard_invariants()` lists the triggers, keys and grants.
- **Grants.** `tally_app` inserts receipts, candidates, dispositions, agencies, agreements, pledges and pledge events, and never updates or deletes them. Stamps are the database's.
- **What stays the core's.** Matching and its confidence threshold; when retention or the service level has run out, and the flags; the disconnection pause and which acts it bars (-26, wave 7); the lapse event's timing; escheat timing; the operator queue and its ordering (oldest first, disconnect-exposed accounts first: WF-U step 2).

## 6. Known limits, stated now

- **L1.** A receipt's candidates are as good as intake made them. The database cannot know a receipt belongs to a customer no one listed. A wrongful disconnection of that customer is outside what the record can prevent.
- **L2.** A pledge's protection under statute (TAC-7.460, "a billing period in which the provider receives" it) is wave 7's law row, not a -24 column. Until that row exists, -26's rule makes the condition unevaluable, which blocks the act (fail toward protection).
- **L4.** The period close's snapshot (-22 S1) does not yet carry a suspense line. Section 2.5 wants one, but the close function is `tally_close`'s SECURITY DEFINER code, and re-issuing it for one line puts -22's whole close back under review. `suspense_held_as_of(tenant, on, recorded)` gives the figure for reconciliation, and the line joins the close when the close is next re-issued.
- **L5.** An agency-paid deposit's return is not yet routed to the agency by the database (section 4.4). Ryan's rule (2026-11-07) governs it; the core pays it to the agency.
- **L3.** An agency remittance batch matched pledge by pledge is the core's. `import_staging.matched_by` gains no `agency_reference` route here (WF-L Exc 2); intake can record the batch line as a receipt or a payment.

## 8. As built (r1, 2026-11-07)

`sql/v5.4.2-24-unmatched-cash-and-pledges.sql`. Where the build differs from, or adds to, the sections above:

- **A payment from a receipt is dated when posted, effective no later than the money came** (section 2.3, settled while writing the freeze test). The first draft dated it on `received_on`, but then money identified after its month was closed could never be posted.
- **The two settings are `tenants.settings` keys** (section 2.4), held to whole numbers above 0 by `tenants_suspense_days_check`, so the history table's known-key list (which -22's and -23's invariants list exactly) is untouched.
- **Locks are advisory, keyed as -23's** (`cash_source_lock_key` with kinds `suspense_receipt`, `agency_pledge`, `agency`). `tally_app` holds no row-lock privilege on these tables. Order: the tenant's close head (the freeze), then the payment's own -23 locks, then the receipt or pledge. Every writer that reads under a lock is pinned to READ COMMITTED.
- **The receipt's cap runs AFTER INSERT** (lesson `projection-checks-lag-in-one-statement`): on payments, and on dispositions, under the receipt's lock. It checks every date a payment or a disposition falls on, so a returned check undone later does not let its money be spent twice.
- **A provider transaction id is held once** by `check_provider_transaction_once`, under an advisory lock on the id, from both tables. A payment made from a receipt carries none of its own.
- **Pledge events carry `event_on`** (withdrawn and lapsed; NULL for recorded in error), so a lapse recorded today reads from the day it took effect. Paid in full outranks an event in the reader, and the event stays on record.
- **`cash_money_agency(payment, credit)`** traces whose money a refund pays out: a payment's own `agency_id`; a credit through overpayment, refund reissue and shortfall reissue. Bounded at 1,000 steps.
- **`import_staging` is not keyed.** It has no `(id, tenant_id)` key and is not this stream's table, so a receipt names its file and row in `evidence_reference`.

## 9. Round 2 (2026-11-07)

`v5.4.2-24-r2-plan.md` lists every change. The design changes:

- **A reversal alone gives nothing back** (section 2.3 changed). -23 lets a bank's take-back be recorded as `reversed`, so the label cannot say whether the money is still held. Money comes back to suspense only by a `misposting_returned` disposition. It names a payment of the receipt that was reversed with no bank return code, is dated on or after the reversal, and happens once. A return code written afterwards is refused.
- **Recorded order** (new). A row that spends a receipt's money is recorded no earlier than every row already on the receipt.
- **Known limits L5 widened, L6 added** (section 6): money in a deposit, and in a credit with no typed source, reads as the customer's.
