# v5.4.2-24 r2 plan (2026-11-07)

Round 1: Opus, Fable and Codex all **not yet**. Codex's eleven probes were run by the author on fresh clones with frozen r1 applied (`tests/v5.4.2-24/review/codex-r1-probes/*.author-run.txt`), and every prediction held. Everything below is settled by a standing rule or a precedent. Nothing is Ryan's call. Item 7 is reported to him because it narrows where his 11-07 rule is enforced.

## Blocking

1. **"Reversed" is trusted to mean posted to the wrong customer.** (Codex B3, Opus B1, Opus B2.) -23 allows a return code on `reversed`, so a bank's take-back recorded as reversed gave the money back to the receipt, and the receipt could be spent twice. The share also came back on every date, not from the reversal's date. **Fix (rule: guard the act, not the label).**
   - A payment from a receipt counts against it on every date unless voided, from the void's recorded time.
   - Money comes back only by a new disposition kind, `misposting_returned`: negative, naming a reversed payment of the receipt (`returned_payment_id`, a two-column key); at most that payment's amount, once; dated on or after the reversal's ledger date and no later than today.
   - It is refused if the payment carries a return code. A return code written later on a payment so returned is refused.
   - The cap and the reader count it as any disposition.
2. **Recorded order.** (Codex B4.) A history load could record a draw before the release it spent, and the reader then showed a negative holding. **Fix (rule: -23's `check_cash_order`).** A payment from a receipt, and a positive disposition, are recorded no earlier than every row already on the receipt: its dispositions, its payments, and a void's recorded time. If the conflicting row committed while this transaction waited (`created_at = now()`), the writer gets 40001 and retries. For a chosen time it gets 23514.
3. **Provider ids.** (Codex B1, B2, B5; Opus B3; Fable B1, B2.)
   - The READ COMMITTED pin goes inside `check_provider_transaction_once`, whenever it checks a positive amount with an id.
   - A row is excluded only within its own table (a receipt and a payment may share a uuid).
   - The check runs on a payment's UPDATE when `provider_transaction_id` is first written.
   - A receipt's payment keeps the receipt's channel, source system and instrument on every update (-23's write-once-later columns may not drift from the receipt).
4. **Clocks.** (Codex B6.)
   - On all seven new tables, `created_at` is finite and no later than `clock_timestamp()` for every role (`enforce_suspense_recorded_at`, after the stamp).
   - The dates are finite: `received_on`, `disposed_on`, `pledged_on`, `event_on`, `effective_from`, `check_date`.

## Should fix

5. **Settings forged through the history table.** (Opus S2.) **Fix (rule: -22's value CHECK on its own key).**
   - `tenant_configuration_history` gets a CHECK on the two keys' values: null, or a whole number from 1 to 9999.
   - `tenants_suspense_days_check` takes the same bound.
   - The reader raises on a value it cannot read rather than casting it.
6. **The invariant checks no constraint, index, required grant or function body.** (Codex S1, Opus S1, Fable S1.) **Fix (rule: -27's exact lists, -23's body checks).**
   - Exact constraint and unique-index definitions for the seven tables and for -24's additions to `payments`, `tenants` and `tenant_configuration_history`.
   - The setup tables' stamp-column UPDATE grants are required.
   - An md5 of the bodies of `check_suspense_receipt_cap`, `check_provider_transaction_once`, `cash_money_agency`, `enforce_payment_suspense_pledge` and `enforce_suspense_disposition`.
7. **The trace's gaps are stated.** (Opus S3, Fable S2, Codex N2.) Known limits:
   - **L5 (widened).** Agency money that becomes a deposit, whether straight away or from a receipt (Fable N5), is returned through -15's deposit events and is not routed by the database.
   - **L6 (new).** A credit of origin `transfer`, `adjustment`, `deposit_refund`, `other` or the rest carries no typed source, so `cash_money_agency` reads it as the customer's money. The core must not move agency money into such a credit until a typed source exists.

   Ryan's rule governs both; the database enforces it on refunds of payments, overpayment credits and the two reissues. Reported to Ryan.
8. **Smaller items:**
   - **Pledge toward a bill.** (Codex N2; inventory L1.) A payment naming a pledge that is toward a bill is restricted to that bill (`restricted_to_invoice_id` equals `toward_invoice_id`).
   - **A retired agency's name can be reused.** (Opus N1, Fable N7.) The name index is partial on `status = 'active'`, matching the retirement message.
   - **A pledge checks its customer's tenant.** (Opus N2.)
   - **Messages.** An invisible pledge on an event says so (Fable N4). The lapse message no longer suggests a no-agreement pledge can gain an agreement (Fable N8).
   - **The header states the retry rule.** Every writer retries 40P01 and 40001 (Opus N4, Fable N3; lesson `lock-order-as-a-rule`). The design's earlier lock-order prose is corrected to the as-built order (Codex N3).
   - **The aged reader's `past_retention`.** NULL while retention is unset, documented as unknown, never false (Codex N2).
