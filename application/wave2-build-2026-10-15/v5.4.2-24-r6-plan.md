## Blocking

1. **An undo typed nsf was left out of the misposted share.** (Opus B1.) Round 5 left out every row typed `nsf`. That included one with a negative amount, which gives money back to the customer. -23's cap lets a negative row sit only under a positive one, and never directly under the posting entry. So a negative nsf row always sits under a reversal and can only be an undo of that reversal. Leaving it out made the share too large: a receipt of 100 whose payment the bank took half of gave back 100, and paid it out again. **Fix (rule: guard the act, not the label; when the database can't tell, keep the money spent).** The walk leaves out only nsf rows that take money (`amount > 0`) and what is beneath them. **Done** (commit 4916ffe): both legs of the walk, and the comment.

2a. **A misposting return dated before a later row under the posting entry counted one receipt twice on the dates between.** (Codex B1, executed by the author.) The payment is reversed naming its first reversal (March 11); that reversal is undone on March 12 and the money reversed again on March 13. The return was checked against the payment's date and the first reversal's date only, so a return dated March 11 was admitted. On March 12 the receipt then held 100 and the payment had still received 100, and a second customer's payment of 100 from the receipt, dated March 12, was admitted: 200 received from one receipt of 100 on that date. The final position was right; the dated one was not, and the receipt's cap is a promise for every date. **Fix (rule: the cap holds on every date; when the database can't tell, keep the money spent).** A misposting return is dated on or after every row under the posting entry, nsf rows included, walked by the link alone. From that date on the tree is at its final net, so the share is right on every date the receipt holds it. The more permissive reading Codex offered (prove the share at the return's date and every later row's date) gains a clerk a back-dated return, at the cost of a second walk; not taken. The message names the last row's date.

3. **No test held the payment bound alone.** (Fable S2.) Fable's shape FA (a reversal, its undo typed nsf, a second reversal) reached 200 under r5's walk, and only the bound at the payment brought it to 100. Under r6 the undo is counted, so FA nets 100 before the bound. It is kept as P14g, a check of item 1. **The bound can no longer be reached with -23's rules in force (rule: a mutation no admitted shape can kill is recorded as equivalent, with the reason, not added).** The reason:
   - -23 records a payment's posting entry at exactly minus the payment's amount, and keeps the net of every entry's tree between 0 and minus its amount, at every date. So the rows under the posting entry net to at most the payment.
   - A positive row's own tree nets between 0 and its amount, by the same rule. So leaving out a positive nsf row and its tree can only lower the sum.
   - So r6's walk is never above the payment. `least(v_pay.amount, …)` stays as a guard in case -23's rule is ever changed. A mutation removing it would survive every shape -23 admits.

   -23's two triggers are `ENABLE ALWAYS`, so this holds in a history load too. A test would need -23's trigger disabled, and that would test -23, not -24.
