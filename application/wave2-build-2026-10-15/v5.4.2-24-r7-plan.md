## Blocking

1. **A misposting return could be recorded before the ledger row that gives its money back.** (Codex B1, confirmed by probe 01.) r6 dates the return after every row under the posting entry, by business date. It did not order it by recorded time. In a database owner's history load, a reversal can be recorded at `clock_timestamp()`, later than its transaction's start, while the payment's move to reversed and a return keep the transaction's start stamp. A read at that start instant then saw the return (the receipt holding 100) without the reversal (the payment still received 100), and a second customer's payment from the receipt, stamped the same, was admitted: 200 received from one receipt of 100, as known at that instant. Today's figures were right; the history at that instant was not, and -22's model says a read at a past instant is fixed once that instant is older than every open transaction. -23's move to reversed checks the lineage's time only when a superuser sets the status time by hand, so the payment's status time doesn't prove the tree was known. **Fix (rule: money is spent, or comes back, only after what supports it is known; the existing order policy):** a misposting return is recorded no earlier than every row under the posting entry, read in the same walk as r6's dates. A return stamped at its transaction's start that meets a later row retries (40001); a chosen earlier time is refused (23514).
