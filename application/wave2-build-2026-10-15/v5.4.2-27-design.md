# v5.4.2-27 design: a credit applied to a bounced payment's shortfall (2026-11-05)

**Authority:** Kyle, 2026-10-30: all of a customer's credits are one pot (GBM `kyle-decisions-2026-10-30-credit-shortfall-after-bounce.md`). Ryan, 2026-11-02: the netting is recorded as a new kind of application pointed at the payment's shortfall, built as its own patch, never as a billed charge (K-10). Ryan, 2026-11-02: -27 goes next after -23 (DECISION-LOG 2026-11-02, -23 r8).
**Closes:** -23's R23 (the database half: the netting has a record) and R24 (the closing act for a credit whose money covers a deficit beside it). AC-44's "record that netting" bullet.
**Migration cost:** additive. A new table and new terms in -23's readers; no existing column changes meaning. Settled by the 11-02 decision ("additive, no existing column changes meaning"), which rules out loosening `invoice_applications` so that a row may name no bill line.

A call marked **(rule)** is settled by a standing rule or a precedent in -23 and reported, not asked. One call was Ryan's (section 6; settled 2026-11-05).

## 1. The case

Kyle's 10-21 example: a customer pays 50, is refunded 30, and the 50 bounces. The bounce waits until the payment's bill applications are undone, so those bills reopen and take money as usual. What is left owed, 30, is money already paid out. It sits on no bill: the payment reads −30 (`payment_position_as_of` remaining). If another payment of the same customer left a credit of 20, the database today still reads that credit as 20, admits its refund and runs its escheat clock (R23). The customer really owes 10.

The same thing happens inside one payment's family (its overpayment credit and the reissues descended from it). There the database already nets at the reading (the cover, `overpayment_absorbed_as_of`), but the credit whose money covers the deficit stays active at 0 forever: its escheat is refused, and it never closes (R24).

-27 gives both cases a row that says "this credit's money paid this payment's shortfall".

## 2. The row

- **A new table, `shortfall_applications` (rule: additive; the 11-02 decision).** Add-only, like `invoice_applications`:
  - tenant, customer;
  - the **target**: the payment whose shortfall is paid;
  - the **source**: a customer credit or a payment (typed FKs, exactly one), as for a bill application;
  - amount, `applied_on` (the money date), `created_at` (the database's stamp);
  - `basis`: `customer_pot` (the core's netting under Kyle's ruling), `operator_override` (reason and user required), or `undo`;
  - `undoes_shortfall_application_id`: a mistake is undone by a negative row naming it, at most its amount; an undo is never undone (rule: -23's A2).
- **Every key carries the tenant** (composite FKs on tenant and id), and the table gets RLS and FORCE RLS like the other cash tables. A single-column key would skip row-level security, the gap rates found on `parent_invoice_id` (board note 11-04).
- **No ledger row (rule).** The ledger is per customer, and the customer's balance is already right: the credit's money and the shortfall net on it. A bill application posts none either. The row changes who holds the money, not how much the customer owes.

## 3. What it may connect

- **Source (rule: the same sources a bill application has; Kyle's "one pot").** An active credit of any origin, or a posted non-deposit payment's own remainder (R16 already says a payment's remainder is applied or refunded directly). A held deposit is not in the pot: it is settled under the deposit rules (-15).
- **Target.** A payment of the same customer that ended by bounce or reversal (`nsf` or `reversed`) and whose family reads a deficit: the payment and its overpayment credits together owe money. Only payment families can carry a deficit. A credit's own entry cannot be withdrawn below what it gave (R15), so no other credit ever reads below 0.
- **Within one family (R24's closing act).** The source may be a credit of the target's own family. The row then turns the cover's netting into a record, and the credit reads 0 by its own draws, not by the cover.
- **Same customer only (rule: X1b).** Money moved between customers is a transfer.

## 4. How it counts

- **On the source:** a new draw, `netted`, beside applied, refunded, given up and carried. It enters every cap the source's draws enter (the credit's own cap, the payment's cap, and the cap on a payment and its credits together), at every date, under -23's recorded-order checks (`check_cash_source`, `check_cash_order`).
- **On the target:** a new term, `repaid`, in `payment_position_as_of`. remaining becomes received − applied − credited − refunded + repaid.
- **The target's cap:** what is repaid never exceeds the family's deficit, at every date. A payment's shortfall is paid down to 0, never above it: an ended payment holds no money.
- **Inside the target's family (rule: -23 r6's split).** A repayment pays the payment's own deficit first, then its credits' deficits, in the order a give-back restores them (the latest share first). The cover at the reading still settles what each line shows, and the lines still add up to the ledger.

## 5. The rest, by -23's rules

- **Freeze (rule: -22 P2, -23).** A row is dated `applied_on` and frozen by the close. The new table joins the close's freeze list and `assert_periods_guard_invariants()`.
- **Locks (rule: -23's order).** Head; then the target payment's lock; then the source's (its payment first when it holds a payment's money). Two nettings in opposite directions can deadlock; every writer already retries 40P01 and 40001. READ COMMITTED only.
- **Leaving money (rule: -23 r9).** A netting row is a move out of the source's family. It is not recorded before a take-back or give-back under that family's payment (`check_cash_leaving_order`).
- **Escheat.** Unchanged in the database. A credit that is netted reads 0 and has nothing to report. Netting across payments before a report stays a per-state rule the core reads (AC-44; Ryan 11-02). Within one family the database already nets.
- **What stays the core's.** Which shortfall and which credit go first; when to net (before a refund, per Kyle); showing one net balance (AC-44). A database refusal of a refund while the customer owes a shortfall stays a later tightening (DECISION-LOG 11-01); -27 does not add it.
- **Invariants.** `assert_cash_guard_invariants()` gains the new table's rules.

## 6. Ryan's call: the shortfall shrinks after it was paid (settled: A, Ryan 2026-11-05)

**The case.** The bounced payment's refund was sent as a check. After a credit has paid the 30 shortfall, that refund check comes back uncashed: the refund moves to `nsf`. The customer never received the 30, so they owed nothing on the bounced payment. The 20 credit should not have been used. Recorded as is, the bounced payment would read +20: money on a payment that has ended, which -23 never allows.

**Options:**
- **A. The returned refund waits until the netting is undone (recommended).** The operator undoes the netting first; the undo and the refund's return go in one transaction, from one screen. This follows Kyle's K-10 2 (a pending refund holds up the bounce until an operator cancels it) and -23's rule that a row taking money away waits while something draws on it. Every step is a recorded act. The cost is that the database refuses the bank's news until the operator acts.
- **B. The netting counts only up to the shortfall at each reading, and the excess reads back on the source.** No refusal. But the source may since have been refunded, escheated or voided, and then the money comes back onto a credit that has ended. -23 spent rounds 6 to 9 on exactly this shape (shares moving after the fact), and each fix needed new order checks.
- **C. The excess stays on the bounced payment.** Breaks -23's rule that an ended payment holds no money; the customer's credit would sit on a dead payment.

**Settled: A (Ryan, 2026-11-05: "Go with option 1, refuse until undone"; DECISION-LOG 2026-11-05).** The rule in the patch: a row that would leave a target payment's repaid above its deficit at any date (the refund's move to `nsf`, `reversed` or `voided` that frees money under the bounced payment) is refused while the netting stands; the undo and the return go together.
