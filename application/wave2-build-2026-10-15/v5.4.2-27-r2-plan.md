## Ryan's call

- **The escheat trap (Opus B2, Fable S1).** Settled by Ryan on 2026-11-05 (option 1; DECISION-LOG). The undo of a shortfall repayment is admitted on an escheated credit. What it gives back is carried, in the same transaction, into a new credit naming the undo. The rule follows -23's `refund_reissue` (10-21):
  - origin `shortfall_reissue`, `customer_credits.reissues_shortfall_application_id`;
  - it posts no ledger row and draws on the escheated credit;
  - its amount is what the undo gave back, as far as it is left on that credit (-23 r5);
  - it is dated on or after the undo, and is never voided;
  - it is checked at commit.
  - It joins the escheated credit's overpayment line where that credit has one, as `refund_reissue` does (-23 r6). The three walks of the line (`reissue_generation`, `overpayment_root`, `overpayment_family`) follow it.
  - Re-issued for it, additions only:
    - `cash_drawn_at`, which gets the carry and the shortfall legs (see below);
    - `customer_credit_position_as_of`, where the new credit's issued is its amount, as a `refund_reissue`;
    - `check_cash_source`:
      - a source credit of origin `shortfall_reissue` has no arrival entry, like a `refund_reissue`;
      - the purpose `shortfall_undo` admits an escheated credit;
    - `cash_source_draws` and `customer_credit_draws_as_of`, which get the carry leg.
  - New, added rather than re-issued:
    - a credit_issued row for the new credit is refused;
    - its key column is fixed;
    - its void is refused;
    - the commit check on the undo.

## By rule (author)

- **The return check's boundary (Codex B1, Opus B1).** r1 checked a move that gives money back only from the target's latest shortfall row's date. A later, unrelated repayment hid an earlier one, so a returned refund was accepted with the bounced payment holding 10 from March 13 to 17 (probe 01, run). In r2, a move giving money back is checked from the target's first repayment's date. In that check, an undo counts from the date of the row it undoes, because it corrects that row. So the operator's undo, dated today (the period may be closed), settles the repayment from its own date. A repayment is still checked from its own date. The positions still read an undo at its own date. The interval between a repayment and its undo is the money the readers show; it is a residual, since the core reads an undo as correcting its row (Fable N3).
- **`cash_drawn_at` (r1: unchanged).** It now counts the shortfall legs and the carry. This is what a family credit held at a take-back's instant (`overpayment_shares`). A carry draws on the escheated credit, and leaving it out would read that credit as holding money.
- **Targets (Opus S1, S2).**
  - A bounced deposit is refused as a target: a deposit is kept out of the customer's pot (K-05).
  - A refund is refused as a target, with its own message.
- **The invariants' privileges (Codex S1).**
  - The app's SELECT and INSERT are each required.
  - The core's SELECT is required.
  - The core is refused REFERENCES and TRIGGER as well as every write.
  - Invariant mutations are added for these.
- **Race harness (Codex S2).**
  - Every wait has a deadline.
  - B's exit is checked before A is waited for.
  - A is killed and reaped on failure.
