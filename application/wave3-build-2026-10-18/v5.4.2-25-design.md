## L: the dispute law family (Q1)

- **L1. `dispute_rules`: a law table, kind `dispute` v1.**
  - Platform-held, written by migration, never edited; the one change is a stamped close above the close floor.
  - **Applicability:** state, service, owner types, system kinds, commission jurisdiction and dates, as `payment_allocation_rules` has them. The area key is `customer_class`: §7.45(6)(D) reaches residential and small commercial customers only. A class the law gives no shelter gets a row saying so, never no row (no row is "unknown law", which refuses).
  - **Seeded from** `law/dispute/dispute_rules.yaml`, emitted by `tools/law/lawc.py`.
- **L2. The document** (`law/dispute/dispute.v1.schema.json`). Envelope `governs: law | delegated_to_utility`, as every kind. The `law` branch has these sections, each closed and each with its own citation. Absent never means "none": an empty list or an explicit `unruled` is written.
  - **`subjects`**, what may be disputed: `{kind: any}`, or `{kind: listed, codes}`. Optional `excluded` codes (CPS's ten). One vocabulary, `dispute_subjects`, covers grounds and exclusions: meter accuracy, rate application, calculation, service not rendered, identity theft, responsible party, deposit, credit, ability to pay, tamper finding, consumption on an accurate meter, other.
  - **`shelter_earned`**, when a dispute earns protection. A union:
    - `before_delinquency` (TX gas);
    - `before_termination` (PA, CA);
    - `within_days`, with days and from `issue | receipt` (Gov't Code §2251: 21 days from receipt);
    - `unruled`.
  - **`must_pay`**, what the customer must still pay while sheltered. A strategy union:
    - `undisputed_charges` (PA, FL, CPS);
    - `average_usage_cap`, with history years, same period, at current rates, and a no-history basis of `similar_customers` (TX gas);
    - `full_bill` (Brenham);
    - `withhold_cap`, with a fraction, where the payer may withhold at most that fraction of the disputed amount (Gov't Code §2251: 1.1);
    - `unruled`.

    Each branch has a component id, which the determination cites.
  - **`shelter_ends`**. A union:
    - `resolution`;
    - `earlier_of_resolution_and_days`, with days and from `issue` (TX gas: 60 from issue);
    - `through_regulator_close` (TX electric, FL);
    - `unruled`.
  - **`stages`**: a list of review stages, each with:
    - an id;
    - the decider: `designated_employee | supervisor | manager | executive | governing_body | regulator`;
    - `file_within`: `{kind: none}` or `{kind: days, days, from: prior_decision | issue | receipt}`;
    - `decide_within`: `{kind: none}` or `{kind: days, days}`;
    - `final` (boolean).

    CPS is two stages (manager, then executive, filed within 30 days and decided within 90, final). *Craft* is one stage (a designated employee with authority to cancel the cutoff).
  - **`blocks`**: what the shelter stops, from the vocabulary `dispute_blocks`: `notice_issue`, `disconnect_order`, `disconnect_execute`, `late_fee`, `collections_referral`, `credit_reporting`. PA blocks issuing a notice; Brenham blocks credit reporting.
  - **`duties`**: from the vocabulary `dispute_duties`: `investigate`, `report_result`, `inform_complaint_route`, `offer_payment_arrangement` (TX gas (6)(D)(i); PA §56.151).
- **L3. Facets:**
  - `subject_codes` (listed and excluded);
  - `block_codes`;
  - `stage_ids`;
  - `must_pay_ids`.

  Each is checked against its vocabulary at insert.
- **L4. The Texas rows** (from the law file):
  - **Investor-owned and cooperative gas, residential and small commercial:** §7.45(6)(D), with `before_delinquency`, `average_usage_cap` (2 years, same period, current rates, similar customers), `earlier_of_resolution_and_days` (60 from issue), one stage, `blocks [disconnect_order, disconnect_execute, late_fee]` and `duties [investigate, report_result, inform_complaint_route]`.
    - **`late_fee` is our reading (rule: fail toward protection, K-CCK).** The text says only that the customer is "not required to pay" the sheltered portion. It neither bars nor allows a late fee on it (law research, part (d)). The row's `source_note` says the reading is ours.
  - **Large commercial and industrial:** `governs: law` with `shelter_earned: unruled` and `must_pay: full_bill`. §7.45 gives these customers no shelter. Written down, so no row is never mistaken for no protection.
  - **City-owned:** `delegated_to_utility`, citing Util. Code §101.003(7) (K-07; memory `texas-muni-outside-gura`).
  - **Governmental payers, any owner type:** Gov't Code §2251.042, with `within_days` 21 from receipt, `withhold_cap` 1.1 and `shelter_ends: resolution`. **Open for the build:** whether the customer-class vocabulary has a governmental class. If it doesn't, this row is a recorded residual and its fixture stays test-only.
- **L5. `dispute_tariffs`: the utility's own policy, kind `dispute_tariff` v1.**
  - The same sections as the law, so the comparator compares like with like.
  - One row per (tenant, state, service, system kind, dates). The key is `[]`, as `payment_allocation_tariffs`. Brenham and CPS each publish one policy; a per-category policy has no source.
  - Tenant-owned under row security; never edited; a stamped close above the close floor (the latest determination citing it). No default row (K-07).
- **L6. Never looser than the law** (`dispute_tariff_compare`). A tariff is refused when written if, for any law row in force for its state, service and owner type:
  - its `blocks` lack one of the law's;
  - its `subjects` exclude a subject the law admits;
  - its `shelter_earned` is narrower (the order: `before_termination` ⊃ `before_delinquency` ⊃ `within_days`);
  - its `shelter_ends` is sooner;
  - its `must_pay` asks for more. `undisputed_charges` asks no more than any other strategy, `full_bill` asks no less, two equal strategies compare their figures, and any other pair is refused as not comparable **(rule: fail toward protection)**.

  Under a delegation there is nothing to compare. Comparing against every row in force is the same as comparing against the most protective one, since the unprotected classes' rows ask for the full bill.
- **L7. Fixtures that never enter real data** (`law/fixtures/dispute-states/`): PA §56.141 and §56.151, FL 25-22.032, CA §10010, TX electric §25.28 and §25.30, and the Brenham and CPS tariffs. Each is validated in CI, and each out-of-scope shape has a fixture showing it is refused explicitly.

## D: the dispute record

- **D1. `disputes`.**
  - **What it names:**
    - tenant and customer;
    - the bill, with a composite FK (invoice, tenant, customer) from -23;
    - or a meter reading, at least one of the two (a read dispute can come before the bill);
    - its `subject` (vocabulary `dispute_subjects`).
  - **When and how it arrived:** `received_on` (the date the customer gave or sent it, chosen by the caller), `channel` (`written | oral | portal | regulator_referral`), and `created_at`, stamped by the database (the recorded coordinate; "recorded_at" below means this column).
  - **Status:** `open → resolved | withdrawn`, forward only. The database moves it, never the app: inserting a `resolved` or `withdrawn` event sets the status, `status_cause_id` (that event) and `status_recorded_at` (its stamp). So the status can't disagree with the events (memory `guards-must-act-not-record`; corrected 2026-10-18 from the -23 pattern of an app-written move naming its cause).
  - **Writes:** identity is write-once from insert; append-only otherwise. The app writes it: an operator takes a dispute.
- **D2. `dispute_amounts`.** Add-only.
  - Each row: the dispute, an amount greater than 0, `determined_by` (`customer_claim | agreed | utility | regulator_interim`), `effective_on`, and `recorded_at` (stamped).
  - Optional `dispute_amount_lines` (bill line, amount), whose sum equals the row's amount. Lines are on the dispute's own bill (composite FK).
  - The amount in force is the latest row by `effective_on`, then recording order, read as of a recorded instant. A revision is a new row (FL: an interim estimate replaces the customer's claim).
- **D3. `dispute_determinations`.** The core's reading of the shelter, written only when the answer changes (memory `design-permanent-record-on-change`). The writer is `tally_app`, with `calculated_by` naming the core version, as `deposit_return_due` is written (the nearest existing decision record; corrected 2026-10-18 from "tally_core"). Each row holds:
  - **What it cites:**
    - the law row, always (a city's delegation row included);
    - the tariff row, required under a delegation;
    - the amount row it read;
    - the core version, the inputs and their fingerprint.
  - **What it found:**
    - `outcome`: `sheltered | not_sheltered | no_terms`. `no_terms` is a city with no tariff row in force, and it shelters nothing in the record. Whether that city still owes *Craft* due process is the core's to evaluate and the reviewer's to act on, not a default.
    - `sheltered_amount` and `must_pay_amount`, the `must_pay` component id that governed, and the figures behind it (e.g. the two-year average and the rates used, inside `inputs`);
    - `shelter_ends_on`, NULL when the end is resolution alone;
    - `blocks`.
  - **Supersession:** a later row names the row it supersedes; one live row per dispute, with the dispute row as the mutex (copied from `deposit_return_due`).
- **D4. What the database checks on a determination** (record integrity, not evaluation; memory `schema-represents-core-evaluates`):
  - **The law row** matches the customer's state, service, the tenant's owner type and system kind, and the customer's class, and is in force on `received_on`.
  - **The tariff row** is the tenant's own, in force on `received_on`, under that law row's state and service.
  - **The component** is one of the cited document's `must_pay` ids.
  - **`blocks`** equals the cited document's list (the tariff's when one applied), so a record can't claim a different shelter than the rule it cites.
  - **Amounts:** `sheltered_amount` is between 0 and the amount row's amount; `must_pay_amount` is at least 0.
  - **No determination after resolution:** once the dispute is resolved or withdrawn, a new determination is refused.
- **D5. `dispute_events`.** Add-only; the app writes them. Each event has a type, a date it occurred on, a `recorded_at` (stamped) and a sequence number.
  - **Types:** `opened` (exactly one, first, written by the database when the dispute is inserted; the app cannot write one), `stage_entered`, `escalated` (to `regulator | governing_body`), `decided`, `resolved` and `withdrawn`. A `stage_entered` or `decided` event needs a live determination, whose document names the stage.
  - **Stages:** `stage_entered` and `decided` name a stage id of the determination's cited document, and the decider's user and role.
  - **Resolution:** `resolved` carries an outcome, `sustained | adjusted | waived`. An `adjusted` outcome names what it produced: a ledger entry (adjustment), a customer credit (origin `dispute_resolution`, which exists today), or a correction bill. The outcome list stays at three plus `withdrawn`, so the meter-test paths Kyle hasn't settled still fit (`adjusted` with a correction, or `sustained`; GBM O14).
  - **One resolution or withdrawal per dispute.** A reopened matter is a new dispute.
- **D6. The reader: `dispute_shelter_as_of(invoice, on, recorded)`.** For each open dispute on the bill as of `recorded`, it returns:
  - the live determination;
  - the sheltered amount, zero once `on` is past `shelter_ends_on` or past the resolution or withdrawal event's date;
  - the blocks.

  Both coordinates are required; NULL raises, with no fallback to live columns (memory `temporal-helper-no-live-fallback`). Per line: `dispute_line_shelter_as_of(line, on, recorded)`, from the amount lines, falling back to a pro-rata reading only when the amount row has no lines, and saying which it used.

  CI-074's failure mode "resolved but the suspension persists" is closed by construction: the reader ends the shelter at the resolution event. Nothing has to be reset.

## T: the two meaning changes

- **T1. `pending_dispute` stops protecting the account (rule: CI-074 and A-15 outrank A-11's seed).** `program_types.is_disconnect_protective` becomes false for `pending_dispute`. Its description points at `disputes`, and new enrollments in it are refused (the program type is kept for history; no row cites it today). `recompute_do_not_disconnect` needs no change: it reads the flag.
- **T2. `meter_readings.dispute_*` go (rule: R-05; a dispute is a record).**
  - The six columns are dropped, and removed from the editable list on consumed reads in `enforce_reading_validation_workflow` (that function is re-created whole).
  - Reading `status = 'disputed'` stays a status. Setting it needs an open `disputes` row naming the reading, and leaving it needs that dispute's resolution or withdrawal.
  - `idx_readings_disputed` is rebuilt on the status alone.

Contract choices made while writing them (by rule, recorded above where they changed the design):
1. **The writer:** determinations are written by `tally_app` with `calculated_by`, as `deposit_return_due` is.
2. **The opening event:** the database writes `opened`.
3. **The status:** the database moves the dispute's status from the resolution or withdrawal event.
4. **`shelter_ends_on` is the last sheltered day, inclusive.** For TX gas that is day 60 counting the issue day: April 29 for a March 1 bill.
5. **No-terms determinations:** `no_terms` carries no must-pay amount or component, nothing sheltered and nothing blocked. `not_sheltered` names its component and shelters nothing.
6. **What a dispute may name:** an issued bill only; it is received no earlier than the bill's or reading's date and no later than today; the reading must be at the dispute customer's location.
7. **Amount lines:** each is positive and at most the line's own charge, so a credit line can't be disputed. The sum is checked at commit.
8. **Per-line reading without lines:** pro rata over the bill's positive lines, reported as `basis = 'pro_rata'`.
9. **The READ COMMITTED pin:** determinations and resolutions are written at READ COMMITTED (25000 otherwise), since the dispute row is the mutex.
