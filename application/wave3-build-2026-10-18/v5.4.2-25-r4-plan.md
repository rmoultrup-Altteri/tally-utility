## Should fix

1. **The reading lock is stronger than it needs to be** (Codex S1, Opus S2, Fable S1; all executed, and Opus and Fable measured the fix). `FOR UPDATE` conflicts with the key-share lock every foreign-key insert naming the reading takes (twelve foreign keys point at `meter_readings`). So a close waits behind an open billing run that has written line items for the reading, and deadlocks against a writer that referenced the reading and then waits for the dispute (Codex probe 07; Opus's photo-plus-amount; Fable P2). **Fix:** `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`. It conflicts with itself and with every plain `UPDATE` of the reading, which is all r3 needed; two closes of one reading still queue and the second is refused (measured by Opus and Fable). Races: keep DR13; add the foreign-key shape (two clerks each open and withdraw a dispute on one reading: both commit, the second refused or accepted by the reading's state, never 40P01) and the close against the reading's own move in both orders (Fable N3's schedules: both end consistent). A mutation back to `FOR UPDATE` must be caught by the foreign-key race (Opus N5: M145 cannot tell the two strengths apart).
2. **A tightened law drops what the utility's policy added** (Opus S1, executed on the battery's own D8f). Under r3, a policy looser than the law in any one respect is not cited, so the record follows the law alone: in D8f the shelter ends on day 60 instead of at resolution, and the policy's own notice block is lost. That is looser than the utility's own promise, which is still in force. **Fix (fail toward protection):** where the law governs and the policy in force is looser in part, the determination cites both, and is checked against their combination: each section is the policy's where it is no looser than the law, and the law's where it is looser (the comparator's own per-section findings decide which); the blocks are both documents' together. So the customer keeps the later shelter end, the smaller amount to pay, and every block either document gives. A policy no looser in every respect is cited as now [D4q]; citing the law alone while a policy is in force is refused. Tests: D8f becomes the combined record (ends at resolution, credit reporting and the notice block both blocked); the law alone and the policy alone are each refused. Mutations on each leg.
3. **A policy may put a stage of its own in front of a stage the law gives** (as built: see below; the after-a-final-stage half was dropped) (Opus S3, Fable S2; executed on the comparator; no seeded law row is affected). A policy-only stage with no decision deadline (or a one-day filing gate) between or before the law's stages can delay the law's stage indefinitely, and a law stage filed "within N days of the prior decision" then counts from the inserted stage. Opus N2 adds: a stage after the law's final stage is admitted, so the law's final decision is no longer final. **Fix (never looser):** the law's stages are matched as the policy's first stages, in order, with nothing between them; a policy-only stage is admitted only after the law's last stage, and only when that stage is not final (the law's final stage stays final). Battery N9g (a policy stage between) moves to the refused side; tests for a stage before, between and after a final stage, and after a non-final one (admitted). Mutations on each.
4. **D4f's own fixture and the dimension mismatches** (Codex S2, the rest of round 2's S2). D4f names a tariff created inside a rolled-back check, so it tests absence. **Fix:** a real other-tenant tariff kept alive for D4f (invisible to the application); separate owner-view tests for a policy of another state, another service and another system; a mutation per comparison.
