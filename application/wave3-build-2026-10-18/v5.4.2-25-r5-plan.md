## Should fix

1. **The combination replaces a list section whole** (Codex B1 cases 1–2, Opus S1, Fable S1; executed on the real path). Under r4, one finding in the stages or subjects section swaps in the law's section whole, so a tightened law removes what the utility's policy adds inside it: the policy's own later appeal (a governing-body stage) becomes unrecordable [D5b], its quicker first decision is lost, and a subject only it admits is no longer admitted. **Fix (fail toward protection):**
   - **subjects:** the union: a subject is admitted if either document admits it, excluded only if both exclude it.
   - **stages:** for each of the law's places, the tariff's stage at that place where it is of the same decider, with the wider of the two filing windows and the quicker of the two decisions; else the law's stage. Then the tariff's own stages after the law's last (what the comparator admits, N9i). Ids: the tariff's at each matched place and for its trailing stages, the law's elsewhere; a duplicate id makes the combination fail closed (a NULL document, refused [D4g]). Each stage keeps its own `final` flag; the law's final decision binds (application contract).
   - **Tests:** D8j rebuilt on a policy with a third, governing-body stage and a 10-day first decision: the combined stages keep both, `stage_entered` at the governing body is admitted on the real path, the regulator's 10 days and the employee's 10 days both stand; a stage at a place of another decider takes the law's; a filing-window case (the policy's open window kept beside the law's quicker decision); a subjects case (deposit kept beside the law's calculation). A mutation back to whole-section replacement for each list, and one per window.
2. **A term that cannot be ranked is not the law's to win** (Codex B1 case 3; Fable N4 and Opus N3 found the same behaviour and called it the floor). The comparator reports "looser *or* not comparable" in one message, and the combination takes the law's term for both. Where the two cannot be ranked (a shelter starting within 20 days of the bill against one starting before delinquency; one ending at the regulator's close against one ending at day 60; a 3-year average cap against a 2-year one), which protects more depends on the customer. **Ruling (Ryan, 2026-11-26): keep both; the core applies the more protective per customer.** **Fix:**
   - the comparator gains a structured form, `dispute_tariff_compare_sections(tariff, law)`, returning one row per finding: its section, whether the tariff is *looser* or the pair is *not comparable*, and the message; `dispute_tariff_compare` is rebuilt on it (same messages, same refusals at a tariff's insert: a policy filed while the law is in force and not comparable is still refused) (Codex's structured-results note; ends the reliance on message prefixes, Opus N3).
   - in the combined document, a section found *looser* takes the law's term; a section found *not comparable* becomes `{"either": [tariff's term, law's term]}`.
   - the determination guard checks the recorded values against one of the alternatives: the must-pay component is either term's id [D4g]; a last day is recorded only under an alternative that ends by a count of days, and no later than that alternative's [D4p]; no last day is recorded only under an alternative that ends by resolution or the regulator's close [D4s]. The shelter's start has no recorded field; both reach the core. The database does not pick; picking per customer is the core's.
   - **Tests:** one D8 case per ranked single section where the law wins (the policy ends at day 30 under a law of 60: a last day of day 60 refused, day 30 admitted [D4p]; the policy's must-pay replaced by the law's smaller ask: the policy's component refused [D4g]) (Opus N6); one per section not comparable (both components admitted, a third refused; a last day under the days alternative admitted, none admitted under the regulator's-close one); the insert-time refusal unchanged. Mutations: the *not comparable* verdict taken as *looser*; each alternative check dropped.
