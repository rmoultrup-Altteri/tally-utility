## C: the decision record (CI-067, CI-069, CI-075)

- **C1. `collections_conditions`, a vocabulary.** The conditions a decision can name, platform-held:
  - **protections:** `dispute`, `payment_arrangement`, `agency_pledge`, `medical_hold`, `military_scra`, `weather_moratorium`, `regulatory_moratorium`, `bankruptcy_stay`, `deceased_hold`, `protected_status`, `tariff_protection` (a utility's own extra protection, Q-6);
  - **bars on the act:** `payment_pending`, `aged_suspense`, `third_party_notice_pending`, `notice_sequence_incomplete`, `arrears_below_floor`, `closed_day`, `elderly_due_date_delay`, `non_disconnectable_debt` (F-1, unregulated debt, carve-outs);
  - **missing rules:** `no_rule_in_force` (K-07: a city with no rule entered).

  Which conditions count for which act in which place is wave 7's rule data (A-14). The vocabulary only names them, so a decision can be read later.
- **C2. `collections_decisions`.** The core writes one row each time it decides whether a collections act may happen. Each row holds:
  - **the act:** `notice` (a disconnection notice), `late_fee`, `disconnect_order` (issuing the order), `disconnect_execute` (carrying it out) or `collections_referral`;
  - **the subject**, by act:

    | Act | Bill | Service location | Meter |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | `notice` | required | required | optional |
    | `late_fee`, `collections_referral` | required | none | none |
    | `disconnect_order`, `disconnect_execute` | none | required | optional |

    **Correction (rule: CCK-7).** The inventory put a notice at bill grain. Kyle's grain table puts a disconnect notice at "meter / service", so a notice names the bill it is for and the service it threatens. A late fee and a referral stay on the bill (dunning_events are per bill).
  - **the evaluation date** (the caller's; CI-075's "evaluation date", never the database's clock);
  - **the outcome:** `eligible`, `blocked` or `unevaluable`;
  - **`active_conditions`** (every bar that was active, CI-069's composition) and **`governing_condition`** (the one credited, for attribution only; C5 in the sources);
  - **`unevaluable_conditions`**: the conditions the core could not evaluate (O3);
  - **`valid_until`**, the last day an eligible decision authorizes an act (inclusive);
  - **its record:** `inputs`, `inputs_fingerprint`, `calculated_by` (the core version), a supersession link, a sequence number, `recorded_txid`, and `created_at` and `created_by`, both stamped.

  Written by `tally_app` with `calculated_by`, as `dispute_determinations` and `deposit_return_due` are. Add-only.
- **C3. What the database checks on a decision** (record integrity, not evaluation; memory `schema-represents-core-evaluates`):
  - **The subject's shape** follows the table above.
  - **The bill** is the customer's (composite FK) and issued.
  - **The service location** is the customer's. For a notice, it is the bill's own location when the bill names one.
  - **A meter** is deployed at the location on the evaluation date (`meter_deployments`).
  - **The outcome's shape:**
    - `eligible` has no conditions of either kind and a `valid_until` on or after the evaluation date;
    - `blocked` has at least one active condition, and the governing one is among them;
    - `unevaluable` has no active condition and at least one unevaluable one. If something is both active and unevaluable, the decision is `blocked`: a bar that is known holds whatever else is unknown;
    - a non-eligible decision has no `valid_until` and holds until the next decision;
    - every condition comes from the vocabulary, with no repeats.
  - **An execute decision holds for its own day only:** an eligible `disconnect_execute` has `valid_until` equal to its evaluation date. **(Rule: fail toward protection.)** EWE is evaluated per day, and GBM's rank 0 refuses a disconnect that relies on an older evaluation.
  - **No future evaluation:** the evaluation date is on or before the database's current UTC date (the -23 convention for a timestamp's date). Otherwise a core could pre-authorize tomorrow's execution on today's facts.
- **C4. One live decision per act and subject.** A first decision for an (act, subject) names nothing. A later one names the live decision it supersedes, under the subject's advisory lock, as `dispute_determinations` do (memory `count-guards-need-lock-handshake`). Its evaluation date is no earlier than the live one's.
- **C5. Idempotent against the live decision (CI-075).** A decision whose evaluation date and fingerprint equal the live decision's is refused: the engine re-ran on the same facts. Different facts on the same date (a payment arrived) give a new row.

  **Correction (rule: memory `append-only-vs-unique-deadlock`).** The inventory sketched a unique key over (act, subject, date, fingerprint) across all rows. That wedges: a payment arrives (inputs A to B), then bounces the same day (B back to A), and the unique key refuses the decision that would restore "eligible" for ever. The check compares against the live row only.
- **C6. Unevaluable blocks (rule: fail toward protection; K-CCK, R-26, R-30).** No law says it; it is a design rule, recorded here and in the vocabulary's comment. Only an `eligible` decision authorizes an act (C7).
- **C7. Acts cite their decision.** Each act names an eligible, live decision for the same act and subject, whose window (evaluation date to `valid_until`) covers the act's date. The act takes the subject's lock first, so a newer decision that commits first is seen and the act is refused. One act per decision.

  | Act recorded as | Decision act | Act's date | Also needs |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `dunning_events` `shutoff_notice_sent` | `notice` | `event_date` (UTC date) | |
  | `dunning_events` `late_fee_applied` | `late_fee` | `event_date` | the late-fee charge citing the decision |
  | `adhoc_charges` of type `late_fee` | `late_fee` | `effective_date` | names its bill (`triggered_by_invoice_id`) |
  | `service_orders` of type `disconnect`, at insert | `disconnect_order` | the insert's UTC date | the order's customer, location and meter match |
  | `dunning_events` `shutoff_scheduled` | `disconnect_order` | `event_date` | the disconnect order citing the decision |
  | `service_order_field_visits` with outcome `executed` | `disconnect_execute` | `arrived_at` (UTC date) | (F1) |
  | `dunning_events` `disconnected` | `disconnect_execute` | `event_date` | the executed visit citing the decision |
  | `dunning_events` `sent_to_collections` | `collections_referral` | `event_date` | |

  The other dunning types (`reminder_sent`, `reconnected`, `payment_plan_created`, `payment_plan_broken`, `written_off`, `resolved`) cite no decision. They are not collections acts against the customer, and none of the sources gates them. The "Also needs" column ties the log to the act it records, so a log line cannot claim a disconnection that no visit performed. This is record integrity (which decision authorized the act), never evaluation.
- **C8. A blocked decision is the bypass trace (O2).** GBM's `bypass_applied` is the `blocked` row itself, so `dunning_events` gets no new types.
- **C9. Rule citations wait for wave 7 (rule: memory `frame-shapes-by-migration-cost`).** No collections rule table exists yet: moratoria, notice sequences, late fees and bypass sets are wave 7. Until then a decision records the rule versions it read in `inputs`, under its fingerprint and `calculated_by`. A citation table with close-floor locks is additive when the first collections rule table lands. Building it now would mean re-creating every rule table's close floor for a citation nothing can make.

## E: hardening what already exists

- **E1. `dunning_events` (CI-075):**
  - customer, invoice and tenant are bound by composite keys `(invoice_id, tenant_id, customer_id)` and `(customer_id, tenant_id)`;
  - a new column `collections_decision_id`, required for the five gated types and absent for the others (C7), bound by a composite key to the decision's tenant and customer;
  - one event per decision (a unique index).
- **E2. The bill's stage is the database's.** `invoices.dunning_stage` moves only when a `dunning_events` row is inserted, and the database moves it (memory `guards-must-act-not-record`). The event's `previous_stage` and `new_stage` are filled by the database, and a caller value that disagrees is refused. A direct write to `dunning_stage` is refused. The mapping is fixed:

  | Event | Stage |
  |---|---|
  | `reminder_sent` | `reminder_sent` |
  | `late_fee_applied` | `late_fee_assessed` |
  | `shutoff_notice_sent` | `shutoff_warning` |
  | `shutoff_scheduled` | `shutoff_scheduled` |
  | `disconnected` | `disconnected` |
  | `payment_plan_created` | `payment_plan` |
  | `sent_to_collections` | `collections` |
  | `written_off` | `written_off` |
  | `resolved` | `resolved` |
  | `reconnected`, `payment_plan_broken` | no move |

  The stage is the latest stage-moving event's. Nothing says which stage follows a reconnection or a broken plan, so those two move nothing (residual R3). The next act moves the stage again.
- **E3. `invoices.late_fee_assessed` and `late_fee_amount` go (rule: wave 2's M1, derive, do not store).** A bill's late fees are its `late_fee` charges (`adhoc_charges` naming it). No function or view reads the two columns (catalog search, 2026-10-19).
- **E4. `customers.do_not_disconnect` is written only by `recompute_do_not_disconnect` (memory `guards-must-act-not-record`).** A customer is born with it false. A change from any statement that is not the enrollment trigger's is refused: the trigger-depth fence `pg_trigger_depth() < 2` (memories `trigger-depth-fence-inside-guard`, `depth-fence-needs-no-temp`). Protection comes from a program enrollment.
  - **For Kyle (not blocking):** the account-detail UI concept edits `do_not_disconnect` as a checkbox (`ui-concepts/components/accounts/AccountDetail.tsx`). After -26 that screen enrolls the customer in a protective program instead.

## F: field verification (CI-071, D9-1)

- **F1. `service_order_field_visits`.** Add-only, one row per visit to a disconnect order. The field tool writes it through the same API as any third-party tool (D3A-1). Each visit records:
  - **arrival:** `arrived_at`, the caller's fact;
  - **as observed:** `captured_meter_serial` and `captured_location_number` (the service point the tech read on site), as `meter_tests.meter_serial_at_test` records a field value;
  - **expected, filled by the database:** the order's location and its `location_number`, the expected meter and its serial. The expected meter is the order's meter, or the one meter deployed at the location on the arrival date when the order names none;
  - **`matched`, computed by the database:** the captured serial and location number equal the expected ones (case and surrounding blanks aside), and the expected meter is deployed at the order's location on the arrival date. So "the address matches but the meter drifted" is a mismatch (axis A14). With no single deployed meter to expect, nothing can match;
  - **outcome:** `executed` or `aborted`. An abort names its reason: `meter_mismatch`, `payment_shown`, `medical_evidence`, `dispute_pending`, `no_access`, `safety` or `other`. PA §56.94 supplies the reasons; for a Texas city they are tariff options, recorded when they happen (memory `launch-scope-is-not-architecture-scope`);
  - **evidence:** `evidence_refs`, a list of references that may be empty.
- **F2. What an executed visit needs:**
  - the order is a disconnect order, not completed, cancelled or superseded;
  - an eligible, live `disconnect_execute` decision for the order's customer and location (and its meter, if the decision names one), covering the arrival date (C7);
  - a match, or a supervisor's `meter_mismatch` approval for this order (F3);
  - at least one evidence reference, or a supervisor's `no_evidence` approval for this order (D9-1);
  - at most one executed visit per order.
- **F3. `service_order_field_approvals`: an override is a supervisor's act (D9-1; CI-071's override failure; memory `guard-the-act-not-the-label`).** Add-only. Each approval names:
  - the order and its kind: `meter_mismatch` or `no_evidence`;
  - a reason code;
  - for `meter_mismatch`, the aborted, unmatched visit whose mismatch it approves and an evidence reference (**rule: R-38 attachment 2**: lifting a protection needs a supervisor and an evidence artifact). The executing visit must then capture the same serial and location number that visit did, so the supervisor approves executing against the meter that was actually seen;
  - **`approved_by`, stamped by the database from the session.** The session must be a supervisor (`session_is_supervisor()`: tenant_admin or platform admin, the -13 meaning). A caller cannot name someone else as the approver.

  Each approval is used by one executed visit at most.
- **F4. A disconnect order is guarded.**
  - **At insert** it cites an eligible `disconnect_order` decision (C7) and cannot be born `completed`.
  - **Write-once:** its type, customer, location, meter and decision. An order cannot be retargeted after its approval, and no order can change into or out of `disconnect`.
  - **Completion:** it reaches `completed` only with an executed visit, and stays there.
  - **Cancellation:** once a visit has executed, the order can only be completed, never cancelled or put on hold.

  Other order types stay as they are.

  **Correction (rule: memory `guard-the-act-not-the-label`, YAGNI).** The inventory added "every other status move names its cause". The regulated act is the disconnection: issuing it, executing it and recording it. Moves between `scheduled`, `dispatched` and `in_progress` are dispatch bookkeeping, and no source asks for their cause. Guarding them would add a cause column nothing reads.

## Lock order (a rule)

A write takes, in this order:
1. **the subject's advisory lock** (`collections_subject_lock`), keyed by (tenant, act, customer, bill, location, meter). It is taken by a decision, and by an act citing one (READ COMMITTED only, 25000 otherwise, as `dispute_lock`);
2. **for a visit, the order's row** (`FOR KEY SHARE`; an order update holds its row lock already);
3. nothing else.

A decision takes only its own subject's lock. An act takes the lock of the decision it cites, read from the decision's row, which is write-once.

## Open for the build (mine, by rule)

1. **How the act's date is read from a timestamp:** the UTC date, as -23 reads `escheatment_events.event_date`. A local-time reading needs a time zone per bill that the schema does not hold for every bill (R1).
2. **Serial comparison:** `upper(btrim(…))` on both sides. A false mismatch is fail-safe; a false match is not, so nothing looser.
3. **Whether `disconnected` needs the executed visit to be its own order's:** yes, through the decision. A visit and an event cite the same execute decision, which names one subject.

## Residuals (expected; the patch header carries the final list)

- **R1.** Act dates are UTC dates. An act late in a Texas evening falls on the next day's date, and so would a decision evaluated then.
- **R2.** Rule citations wait for wave 7 (C9). Until then the decision's rule versions are in its `inputs`.
- **R3.** `reconnected` and `payment_plan_broken` move no stage.
- **R4.** Whether a city owes *Craft* due process before a disconnect is the core's evaluation, recorded as a condition. The database does not infer it.
- **R5.** The `do_not_disconnect` fence is a trigger-depth fence. It holds while tally_app can define no code (TEMP revoked; memory `template-clone-drops-acl`).
