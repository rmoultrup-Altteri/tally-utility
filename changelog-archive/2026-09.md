## 2026-09-22 (later) — A-2 drafted, reviewed, and held: the trim was theatre, the gate asked the wrong question, and two rulings are missing

**What was done:** `sql/v5.4.2-12-backbilling-caps.sql` drafted (1,993 lines), run past two independent adversarial reviewers, revised once, and **committed unlanded** (`ef27cb1`). Not mirrored into `tu.sql`. Two questions block it with Kyle.

**What it builds.** 16 TAC §7.45 limits how far back a utility may *bill* a customer it under-charged, and separately how far back it may *enforce collection* — the two diverge, and for a non-registering meter three months may be billed and none of it ever enforced. `backbilling_cap_rules` carries both bounds with explicit non-null scope enums on each side (R-20 ruled this for the enforceable side against the NULL-poisoning class; F-7 found the billable side had the same three-shape problem and no discriminator, where a NULL read as "no limit" bills past statutory authority). Texas gas seeds for *both* customer classes — the unprotected rows written explicitly `uncapped` rather than left absent, so a missing rule stays an error rather than a silent permission. `backbill_cause` + `anchor_date` on the correction target, findings-based per R-19, with the under-reach override in **database-stamped write-once columns** rather than `invoice_events`, which `tally_app` can write itself. Two append-only tables: `correction_run_target_events` for the setup-time decisions that predate any correction invoice, and `backbilling_period_evaluations` for R-25's per-period evidence. The R-30 read classification as a platform-fixed function, not a table — a tenant able to reclassify `ami_offline` as beyond its control would be granting itself a disconnection right the rule does not give. `tenants.regulatory_class_mode` ships CCK-14's simple default with the volumetric mode declared but **refusing** rather than silently degrading. The v5.4.2-10 target freeze extended to the two new columns (F-4, an amendment to R-19 stated in the header). The AC-32 tail, shown to RAISE on four planted drifts.

**Round 1 found four critical/high defects. All are fixed; two were structural.**

**The trim was recorded and never applied.** The evaluator computed the forfeited amount, wrote it to the permanent evidence row, raised a NOTICE announcing it — and the invoice then issued at the *full* delta, because the gate only refused when the trim consumed everything. By this patch's own header standard ("a guard that fails open is worse than none because it is trusted") that is the worst available outcome: the log said forfeited, the bill was not. It also had no fixed point — the trim is proportional to a delta derived from the draft, so re-drafting at the trimmed figure re-prorates the already-trimmed number ($31 → $9 forfeited; redraft at $22 → $6.39). Now refused outright pending Q-C.

**`amount_due` is caller-set and tied to nothing — found independently by both reviewers**, which by `reviewer-fixes-need-measuring` makes it near-certain. Nothing in 21,916 lines derives it from `invoice_line_items`. Every arithmetic test in the gate read it on both sides of the comparison. **The first fix was the same defect mirrored:** told amount_due could not be trusted, the revision read only the line items — but `void_invoice()` reverses exactly `-(amount_due)`, so that column reaches the ledger whatever the lines say, and a correction with honest $105 of lines and `amount_due` at $5,000 issued clean. Reproduced by the author before it shipped. The measure is now the **greater of the two paths**. Requiring the two to be *equal* was tried and **rejected on measurement**: batteries 10 and 11 build invoices with `amount_due` 0 against non-zero lines, and equality refused them — confirmed as the cause rather than test contamination before the reasoning was written into the patch.

**The gate asked what the bill was called, not what it did.** Keyed on `invoice_type = 'correction'`, it was walked past three ways: a `duplicate` and a `credit_memo` may both legally carry `replaces_invoice_id` (and v5.4.2-10 *explicitly exempts both* from its coordinate binding), and — needing no `replaces_invoice_id` at all — voiding a bill and issuing a fresh `regular` invoice for the same premise and period. The patch had quoted R-22's "the regulated act is the charge, not the void" to justify keeping the gate out of `void_invoice()`, then keyed the gate on a type label one function later. Re-keyed on the facts: was this premise and period billed before, and does this bill charge more. A rebill that charges *less* still passes.

**The target's identity was unbound to the bill it corrects.** `meter_id` and `customer_id` are plain FKs and both choose the cap — a sibling meter at the same address with an older test date lengthens the window, and under `explicit_class` naming a large-commercial customer on a residential customer's bill resolves `unprotected`, which is billable-uncapped. Both now bound to the voided bill. The evidence row also records the meter and the `last_test_date` that produced its window, because that field is mutable and the window was otherwise not re-derivable — which is the entire purpose of the record.

**Two questions now block, recorded in GBM `2da510b`** (`kyle-questions-2026-09-22-a2-straddle-and-last-test.md`). **Q-C, the straddling period:** R-25 makes the billing period the unit and R-23 forfeits the remainder; between them they settle only a period wholly inside the window and one wholly outside it. With monthly periods and a mid-month window the straddle is the common case, not an edge case.
