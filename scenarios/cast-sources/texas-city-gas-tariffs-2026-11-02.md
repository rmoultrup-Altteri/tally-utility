# Texas municipal gas systems: published tariff material

Research for modelling an invented test cast on real tariff structure. Everything here was retrieved on **2026-11-02**. Every fact carries its source, given as a bracketed key that points to the source list at the end. "Not found" means I searched and found nothing published. It does not mean the city has no such rule.

## 1. Brenham (Washington County): the larger system

- **Size:**
  - The city's page says the system serves "more than 5,100 customers" and has 141 miles of main [BR-PU].
  - A search snippet of the same page earlier quoted "more than 4,100", so the page has been updated.
  - The Railroad Commission lists 134.00 miles [RRC-OPS].
  - A 2018 city report ranks it about 5th in sales among the state's 89 city-owned gas systems [BR-2018].
  - This is at, or just over, the top of your range.
- **Source quality:** This is a real tariff book: section 500, sheets 510–595, effective **October 1, 2026**. It supersedes the rates of 10/01/2022 [BR-TAR]. The tapping sheet is dated 10/01/2022 and the gas cost sheet 10/13/2021. **Current.**
- **Customer classes (rate schedules), split by inside the city ("urban") and outside it ("rural"):**

  | Schedule | Class | Who qualifies | Customer charge (per month) | Distribution charge (per Mcf) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | G-A | Residential, urban | maximum load 350 cubic feet per hour or less | $12.81 | $2.962 |
  | G-F | Small commercial, urban | 350 cubic feet per hour or less | $12.81 | $2.962 |
  | G-B | Large commercial and small industrial, urban | over 350 cubic feet per hour | $72.08 | $2.013 |
  | G-C | Large industrial, urban | over 36,500 Mcf per year | $70.54 | $1.838 |
  | G-D | Residential, rural | outside city limits | $14.73 | $3.406 |
  | G-G | Small commercial, rural | outside city limits | $14.73 | $3.406 |
  | G-H | Large commercial and small industrial, rural | outside city limits | $82.89 | $2.314 |
  | G-E | Governmental | Brenham ISD, Washington County, Blinn College, State of Texas, the US government, and city departments | $72.08 | $1.670 |

  Every schedule also carries a **commodity charge of $5.35 per Mcf** and a monthly gas cost adjustment [BR-TAR].
- **Inside vs outside the city:** Outside customers pay about 15% more on both the customer charge and the distribution charge. Residential goes from $12.81 to $14.73, and from $2.962 to $3.406 per Mcf [BR-TAR].
- **Minimum bill:** "The minimum monthly base bill shall be the customer charge for each month of the year, regardless of whether service is disconnected during the summer months." In other words, the customer charge is still billed when a customer has the gas turned off for summer [BR-TAR].
- **Volumetric unit and blocks:** Mcf, as a flat rate per Mcf. There are no declining blocks. Class is decided by connected load (350 cubic feet per hour) or annual volume (36,500 Mcf) [BR-TAR].
- **Gas cost mechanism (sheet 595):**
  - The $5.35 commodity charge is a *fixed estimate* of the gas cost per Mcf.
  - The gas cost adjustment reconciles that estimate to the actual market price. It can be **positive or negative**, and it "is reviewed and may be adjusted monthly."
  - It has two parts. One looks forward: the city's estimate of next period's cost. The other is a true-up for the prior period's over- or under-collection.
  - The city takes no markup on gas.
  - Over- and under-collected balances are kept in a separate fund and reported as deferred inflows or outflows at fiscal year-end [BR-TAR].
  - The city's news release explains that the adjustment had been a *credit* while market gas was cheaper than $5.35, and became a *charge* once gas rose above it (October 2021) [BR-NEWS].
- **Billing and late penalty:**
  - Bills are due 15 days after issue. A due date on a weekend or holiday rolls to the next workday.
  - "All rates specified herein are net, and the gross rates for delinquent payments are ten percent (10%) higher." So the 10% penalty is expressed as a gross price versus a net price.
  - If the bill is unpaid on the due date, *all* the customer's utility services become delinquent and can be disconnected [BR-TAR] [BR-CS].
  - The length of the billing cycle is not stated.
- **Taxes:** Customers are "billed for all taxes applicable to the sale of natural gas, excluding gross receipts taxes" [BR-TAR]. Which taxes apply is not listed.
- **Deposits (rate tariff section 1005, as quoted on the billing page):**
  - Homeowner $150.
  - Renter $250.
  - Non-owner without electric or gas $145.
  - Contractor, each new construction $100.
  - Landlord $300.
  - Commercial: twice the highest monthly bill, with a $300 minimum.
  - A residential deposit is refunded as a bill credit after 12 months if the customer has had no collection activity: no returned checks, no disconnect notices, and no more than 2 late penalties. It is also refunded if no bill is past due.
  - At disconnection, the deposit is applied to the final bill.
  - Interest on deposits is not stated [BR-CS].
- **Fees:**
  - Turning service off or on at the customer's request: $50, plus $25 after hours.
  - Pilot relight: $15, or $30 after hours.
  - Tapping (schedule G-K): $300 for a 1-inch tap with service line, meter and regulator for the first 100 ft. $440 for 1¼-inch. $1,140 for 2-inch with a 275 cubic-feet-per-hour meter. Actual cost at 4 inches and up.
  - Main or service extension: $3.15 or $3.45 per ft.
  - Meter set on a pre-stubbed service: $80.
  - Upsizing a meter from 1 inch to 1¼ inch: $230 [BR-TAR].
  - Returned check fee and meter test fee: not found.
- **Payment plans and seniors:** An "Average Monthly Payment Plan" form exists, but its terms are not published. There is a senior *sanitation* rate, but no senior gas provision. Nothing was found on medical or cold-weather protection [BR-CS].
- **Mid-cycle rate change:** Not stated. The tariff states its effective date as a calendar date ("October 1, 2026"). The 2021 news release ties the change to "the October utility billing cycle," which suggests the new rates apply to the whole bill [BR-NEWS].
- **Railroad Commission pipeline safety fee on the bill:** Not found in the tariff.

## 2. Fort Stockton (Pecos County): mid-size, priced off a supply index

- **Size:**
  - 122.20 miles of main [RRC-OPS].
  - Meter count not found. The town has about 8,000–8,500 people, which suggests roughly 2,500–3,500 meters, but that is my estimate, not a published figure.
- **Source quality:** Code of Ordinances chapter 24, section 24-51(c), on Municode (code version of Apr 30, 2026) [FS-MUNI]. Ordinance 25-118 was passed 08/11/2025 [FS-ORD]. **Current**, and changed several times a year: the amendment history runs through ordinances 25-115, 25-118 and 25-125 in 2025.
- **Customer classes:** In effect one gas class. The same rate applies "within and without corporate limits," and anyone "not specifically enumerated" is charged a rate set by the city manager from estimated volume [FS-ORD]. Water is the service that has separate residential, commercial/governmental, hotel and prison classes.
- **Rate structure (unusual and useful):**
  - The "minimum bill" is the monthly West Texas Gas index price **plus a "marginal service adjustment" for the first Mcf**. Each further Mcf is billed at the index plus a smaller adder.
  - The adders move year by year:
    - Ordinance 25-118 (August 2025): $23.39 for the first Mcf, then $2.04 per Mcf [FS-ORD].
    - Municode text, showing ordinance 25-125 of 9/8/2025: $24.10 for the first Mcf, then $2.11 per Mcf [FS-MUNI].
    - The city's gas department web page today: $26.10 for the first Mcf, then $2.21 per Mcf [FS-WEB].
  - The web page figure is presumably a later ordinance that Municode hasn't picked up yet. **Three versions of the rate are published at once** — a good real-world case for keeping each rate with its effective dates.
- **Gas cost mechanism:**
  - The index price is passed through monthly, per Mcf.
  - The ordinance also describes a *future* formula: the blended cost of gas from West Texas Gas and an alternate supplier, including transportation, × **1.05 for unaccounted-for gas**, + **$3.10 cost of service**.
  - Worked example in the ordinance: an average cost of $4.13 × 1.05 = $4.33, + $3.10 = **$7.43 per Mcf** [FS-ORD].
- **Inside vs outside the city:** Same rate for gas. Water and sewer outside the city are 1.5 times the inside rate [FS-ORD].
- **Billing and penalty (section 24-52):**
  - Meters are read monthly.
  - The bill is due at city hall by the **10th** of the month after the reading.
  - If unpaid on the **20th**, a **10% penalty** is added, plus a **$50 service-order charge**, and service can be disconnected.
  - A meter re-read costs $25 if the original reading was correct, and is free if it was wrong [FS-MUNI].
- **Deposits (section 24-66 area):**
  - Residential deposits are tiered by credit rating: excellent $125, good to moderate $200, poor $275.
  - Renters: a **non-refundable** $275 that is applied to the final bill.
  - Commercial: an estimate of 2 months' billing. A related account under the same business name pays a $50 non-refundable deposit [FS-MUNI].
  - Section 24-67: the deposit is applied to the final bill and the balance refunded [FS-MUNI].
  - Interest on deposits: not stated.
- **Fees:**
  - Temporary (voluntary) disconnect $10 and reconnect $35. Nothing is billed while the meter is locked, but it is still read monthly, and any usage is billed on reconnection (section 24-41).
  - Returned check $35 (section 24-68).
  - Gas taps: free for a 250–275 meter; actual cost for larger settings. Relocating a meter is $250 when it needs a new tap, or $100 for a service extension up to 10 ft [FS-MUNI] [FS-ORD].
  - **"Gas Incentive Program": a $400 credit to the account when new service is opened** [FS-ORD]. This is a real one-off credit line on the bill.
  - Annual gas leak survey required for facilities housing children or inmates (section 24-54) [FS-MUNI].
- **Seniors, payment plans, weather and medical protections:** Not found.
- **Taxes and the Railroad Commission fee on the bill:** Not found.
- **Mid-cycle rate change:** Not stated. The ordinance takes effect on adoption and publication.

## 3. Sealy (Austin County): about 1,300 customers, billed in Ccf

- **Size:**
  - About **1,300 gas customers**, as reported in 2021 coverage of the February 2021 winter-storm gas bill of $2,064,000. A normal month's gas purchase was $70,000–$100,000 [SEALY-KWHI].
  - 57.10 miles of main [RRC-OPS].
- **Source quality:** Code chapter 102, article II, division 3 "Natural Gas", on Municode. Division 3 was rewritten by ordinance 2021-28 (12/15/2021) and amended by ordinance 2025-06 (2/18/2025) [SEALY-MUNI]. **Current.**
- **Rate structure:** Three parts: a fixed monthly charge by class, a base volumetric charge **per Ccf**, and a **fuel adjustment factor per Ccf that "adjusts monthly to pass-through the actual cost of gas"** under the supply contract [SEALY-MUNI].
  - Fixed charges per month: residential $11.60, commercial $40.00, industrial $600.00, governmental $58.20.
  - Volumetric charge per Ccf: residential $0.88, commercial and governmental $1.14.
  - Industrial is priced by **monthly-usage tier**:
    - Small, 25,000 Ccf or less: $0.83.
    - Medium, 25,001–50,000 Ccf: $0.59.
    - Large, 50,001–100,000 Ccf: $0.59.
    - Extra large, over 100,000 Ccf: $0.59.
  - These tiers place a customer in a class; they are not blocks inside one bill.
  - "The city no longer allows industrial contracts"; existing contracts are terminated [SEALY-MUNI].
- **Several units on one meter:** The fixed charge is multiplied by the number of units, "whether occupied or not" [SEALY-MUNI]. Good test-cast material.
- **Inside vs outside the city:** No outside-city gas rate found.
- **Billing and penalty (section 102-74):**
  - Due on the **1st** of the month.
  - **10% penalty** if paid after the **10th**.
  - Delinquent if unpaid on the **20th**.
  - The city manager may waive penalties case by case [SEALY-MUNI].
- **Disconnection (sections 102-75 and 102-107):**
  - Service is disconnected on and after the date the bill becomes delinquent.
  - A **$30 administrative fee** applies to any delinquent balance of $10 or more, whether or not service is cut.
  - Reconnecting after hours or on holidays costs $30.
  - A **$15 special charge for summer shut-off and winter turn-on**.
  - At least **7 days' notice** of termination, with a right to a hearing before the utility office manager and an appeal to council [SEALY-MUNI].
- **Deposits (section 102-73, paid in cash):**
  - Owner-occupied residential $75.
  - **Senior citizen (65 or older) $40**.
  - Tenant, mobile home or manufactured home $300.
  - Commercial: 1½ to 2 times the estimated monthly bill, minimum $250.
  - The deposit is refunded only when the customer gives notice to disconnect. The city manager may adjust deposits.
  - Interest on deposits: not stated [SEALY-MUNI].
- **Connection:** A 1-inch residential tap (up to 100 ft, with meter and regulator) is $1,200. Boring is $30 per ft when the main is across the street. Larger or longer jobs are charged at actual cost [SEALY-MUNI].
- **Payment plans:** After the February 2021 storm the city offered payment plans and suspended automatic debit for gas so that no one was auto-drafted for an unexpectedly large bill [SEALY-KWHI]. These were one-off measures, not code. No standing plan was found.
- **Taxes, the Railroad Commission fee, and mid-cycle rate changes:** Not found.

## 5. Brady (McCulloch County): reference only, no longer city-owned

- **Status:** The system was sold to West Texas Gas Utility, LLC, which has operated it since **10/01/2025** [BRADY-SALE]. A Railroad Commission bulletin of 11/14/2025 covers the acquisition (seen only in a search snippet). The code's fee schedule still shows gas rates, with an amendment history that includes ordinance 1405 of 11/18/2025 [BRADY-A10]. So the published code lags reality. **Do not treat these as live rates.**
- **What is worth borrowing for structure:**
  - **Cost of gas defined with a line-loss factor (section 13.06.001, ordinance 693, 1992):**
    - The cost of gas = the city's actual cost per Mcf at the city gate in a month × (gas delivered to the city gate over the prior 12 months ÷ gas sold over the prior 12 months).
    - The monthly gate cost totals purchase, production from city-owned wells, gathering, storage and transmission, plus or minus revenue from gas sold to others, divided by the volume delivered [BRADY-1306].
  - **Class decided by 12 months of history (section 13.06.002):**
    - A customer is industrial if they average 4 Mcf a month or more over the preceding 12 calendar months.
    - A new customer can start on the industrial rate if they agree to repay the difference if their first-year average turns out below 4 Mcf [BRADY-1306].
  - **The last published rates (section A10.004):**
    - Residential: $9.00 service charge per meter, $5.00 per Mcf distribution, plus the cost-of-fuel adjustment, with a $10.00 minimum bill.
    - Commercial: $9.50, $5.00 per Mcf, $10.00 minimum.
    - "Transmission fee $0.97 per mcf" (the class is unclear).
    - Industrial: $150 service charge per meter, with fuel "equal to the rate charged to the city for all gas purchases times the customer's gas consumption" [BRADY-A10].
  - **Pilot relight:** **free for customers 60 or older** during business hours, and $25 for those 59 or younger. After hours it escalates: 1st time $25, 2nd $35, 3rd $45 [BRADY-A10]. This is a real **age-based fee waiver**.
  - **Deposits:** Residential gas $50. Commercial set by the finance director. The schedule also lists "Two utilities: $30.00; Three utilities: $40.00", whose meaning is unclear [BRADY-A10].
  - **Other fees:** Temporary service up to 10 days: $15 per utility. Transfer fee $10 per utility. Late fee 10%. Turn-on or turn-off $50. Reconnect $50, or $60 after hours. Re-read $5 [BRADY-A10].
  - **Due date:** Bills are "due and payable upon receipt", rolling to the next business day if that is a Sunday or holiday. Elsewhere: due "a minimum of fifteen (15) days after bill rendering", then a late penalty. Notice of discontinuance is 5 days [BRADY-1303].
  - **Average monthly payment plan:**
    - Residential only, after 12 months at the same address, with a zero balance and no more than 2 late payments in 12 months.
    - The plan amount is the average of the last 12 bills, and the over/under balance is tracked on each bill.
    - A **true-up to zero is due with each March bill**.
    - Enrolment is in March or July; plan billing starts in April or August [BRADY-1303].

## State-level facts that apply to all of them

- **Railroad Commission pipeline safety fee (16 Texas Administrative Code §8.201).**
  - Operators pay an annual fee of up to **$1.00 per service line**, counted from the federal pipeline-safety annual report.
  - They may recover it as a **flat, one-time annual surcharge** of no more than $1.00 per service line.
  - It may be billed only after the fee is paid, and **may not be billed to a state agency**.
  - A report is due within 90 days of the last billing cycle that carries the surcharge.
  - Amendments adopted in 2025–26 extend the fee to propane systems (House Bill 4042) [TAC-8201] [TGS-PSF].
  - None of the city tariffs above mention it, so whether city systems bill it is a per-utility choice.
- **Sales tax on residential gas.**
  - Residential gas is exempt from state sales tax (Tax Code §151.317).
  - A city may elect, under Tax Code §321.105, to impose *its local* sales tax on residential gas and electricity together.
  - Some small cities have done so; Tom Bean's 2006 ordinance is one example, and Chico's code has a section on it [TAX].
  - So "city sales tax on residential gas" is a yes or no for each city. That is exactly what Brenham's "all applicable taxes" line leaves open.
- **Gross receipts tax.** Brenham explicitly excludes gross receipts taxes from the bill [BR-TAR]. I did not verify whether the state gas utility gross receipts tax applies to city-owned systems.

## Structural patterns

**What nearly all of them share**
1. **Flat price per unit plus a monthly gas-cost pass-through.** None uses declining blocks inside a bill. Where volume tiers exist (Sealy's industrial tiers, Brenham's 350 cubic feet per hour and 36,500 Mcf lines, Brady's 4 Mcf average), they **decide the customer's class** rather than pricing slices of one bill.
2. **The gas cost is monthly and per unit of volume,** and is not fixed in the ordinance. The ordinance fixes the *method*, never the number. That number is the city's own reference value, and it changes every month.
3. **A fixed monthly charge or a minimum bill,** often billed even when the customer has shut off for summer (Brenham explicitly; Sealy's summer shut-off and winter turn-on fee).
4. **A 10% late penalty** on every one of the five. The day it attaches is what differs: issue + 15 days, the 1st, the 10th, or the 20th.
5. **Deposits that vary by owner versus renter or by credit,** with **no interest stated anywhere** and rules that reapply the deposit to the final bill.
6. **Gas billed on a combined city utility bill,** with delinquency on one service putting all services at risk (Brenham states this outright).
7. **Rates change by ordinance, often several times a year,** on a calendar effective date. **None of them publishes a rule for proration or for a rate change in the middle of a billing cycle.**

**Where they differ (the dimensions the test cast should span)**
- **Billing unit:** Mcf (four of the five) versus **Ccf (Sealy)**. The volumetric rates are not comparable without converting.
- **How the gas cost is expressed:** a fixed estimate plus an adjustment that can go either way, with a true-up (Brenham); the supply index passed straight through (Fort Stockton, Stinnett, Sealy); or a formula with a 12-month delivered-over-sold loss ratio (Brady), or a 5% loss allowance (Fort Stockton).
- **Fixed-charge style:** a separate customer charge (Brenham, Sealy, Brady); a first-unit charge (Fort Stockton); or only a minimum bill (Stinnett).
- **Number of classes:** from one (Stinnett, Fort Stockton) to eight (Brenham, including governmental and urban/rural).
- **Outside-city pricing:** a separate, higher schedule (Brenham, about 15%); the same rate (Fort Stockton); or not addressed.
- **Collection timetable:** a due date relative to issue (Brenham) versus fixed calendar days (Stinnett 1st and 10th, Sealy 1st/10th/20th, Fort Stockton 10th and 20th). There are also extra one-off charges at delinquency: Fort Stockton $50, Sealy $30 admin, Stinnett $20.
- **Senior provisions,** where they exist at all, are small and specific: a lower deposit (Sealy, 65+) or a waived fee (Brady, 60+). No disability, medical or cold-weather provisions were found in any city's text.
- **Unusual items:** Sealy's charge multiplied by the number of units on one meter, occupied or not. Fort Stockton's $400 new-service credit and its non-refundable renter deposit. Stinnett's extra deposit after a second disconnect. Brenham's tracked over/under fund. Brady's average-payment plan with a March true-up.
