## Findings

### Should-fix

**1. SCN-0166, the Open for Ryan (line 761) and its Sources (line 750): the question is not framed with what Ryan needs to decide it.** Six gaps:

- (a) *Whose rule it is.* The Open cites "a refund is never held back (SCN-0140)". SCN-0140 itself marks that as "Kyle 11-03, answer 8; this stream's reading" (line 259). Kyle's words are "A missing rule never holds back a refund", said about how far back a refund reaches when a city's ordinance is silent (GBM batch-1 file, line 22), not about whether a correction waits for the next bill. The Open should say that the timing rule is this stream's extension, adopted as a standing rule, so Ryan sees he is ruling on its reach, not applying Kyle.
- (b) *The other side is already a utility setting.* SCN-0141 settled that catch-up or rebill is the utility's stated method, catch-up the default (line 281). So the real question is whether the standing rule overrides a utility's stated catch-up method once an actual reading shows the customer overbilled. The Open calls it "the catch-up default" only.
- (c) *One example is not run through both options.* The rebill option shows November ($28.53, credit $22.94) and "December would run from 701.5", but not December's bill. It is 4.5 Mcf: $14.75 + $15.35 + $24.08 + $2.61 = **$56.79**. Totals: catch-up $51.47 + $33.43 = **$84.90**; rebill $28.53 + $56.79 = **$85.32**. Correcting now gives BB-28 $22.94 back 26 days sooner and costs 42¢ more overall, because 2.5 Mcf moves from November's $0.42 to December's $0.58. Ryan should see both numbers.
- (d) *The worked case is the mildest one.* In (b) December's actual is above the billed estimate, so "no credit line is needed". The sharper case is an actual below it (say 703.2 on Dec 15): a credit bill a month late, which is SCN-0141 (b′)'s open question with Kyle. Add it, or say it is left out.
- (e) *What "correct now" means is not stated.* The Nov 13 reading is not a Nov 15 reading: is the rebilled period closed at Nov 13 (two days short, full customer charge, as the alternative line assumes), and is November then no longer an estimated month (run of estimates 0; under (b′) December would be the first estimate, 705.5)? A test cannot be built for the second option as written.
- (f) *Two facts that bear on it are missing.* At Bluebonnet an estimated bill can ground a cut-off (`cast.md` §6), so under (b′) BB-28 can be cut off for a bill the utility's own reading shows is too high. And where Texas binds, §7.45(2)(D)(iv) names "an inaccurately estimated bill" beside "an incorrectly read meter" as "the utility's error" (rule text, lines 150 – 153); that is not binding on Bluebonnet, but it is the nearest thing to a source for treating an over-estimate like SCN-0140's keying error. Also worth one line: under-estimates stand (SCN-0160) and over-estimates would not, which matches Kyle's "split by direction" (answer 8).

A middle option exists and should be offered: the reviewer chooses, with the choice and reason recorded, as Kyle ruled for a read keyed too low (answer 7).

The provisional result itself is consistent with SCN-0141 and SCN-0160: same figures as SCN-0160 ($51.47, $52.11), the billed estimate stands, the next actual bills from the estimated reading, the back-dated actual does not end the run.

**2. SCN-0165, last bullet but one (line 742): "resolved at review, by an override with a reason or by rejection" would, as built, validate the wrong reading.** An override is the disposition that lets a reading through (`APPLICATION-CONTRACTS.md:231`: dispositions are estimate accepted, manual read entered, read corrected, field order dispatched, excluded, override). Overriding the review on 1,606.1 makes it a validated actual dated May 1, the same day as the May estimate; as built a same-day estimate beside an actual is not counted (AC-21, line 236; SCN-0158 (b)), so the run of estimates would end, contradicting the Rule ("It ends nothing"), and June's usage would be measured against a validated 1,606.1. Fix: in (a) and (b) the held reading is resolved by excluding it (or, in (c), by "read corrected" naming the replacement, which is built); say an override is not offered here, or that the core refuses it for a reading that cannot be true.

**3. (ii) The reason "typo correction" does not fit a new observation; Kyle's rule keeps typo fixes for editing an un-billed reading in place (SCN-0139's Rule, D-5), and the card's reading is un-billed. A second reading after a failed high check is "re-read for high or low usage" (SCN-0139 (a)). (iii) A reading given by phone is not the postcard of §7.45(6)(C); say it is treated as a customer reading the utility asked for, this stream's reading, alongside Kyle's "no customer-initiated reads in v1" (GBM `wu5-wu6-kyle-decisions-2026-07-10.md:119`).
