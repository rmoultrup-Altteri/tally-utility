The position then reads *less* than the caps admit (B1, executed, two shapes). No money is at risk and no position ever reads more than its cap admits; this is the opposite direction to round 6's B1. The fix I recommend is to the claim and the test, not to the cover: the general statement is unattainable with per-side caps, and the true invariant (position ≤ cap; the positions add up to the pool; no credit reads money beside another's deficit) should be the one the header, R17/R20 and agree() state. ## Blocking

### B1. A position equals what its cap admits only while one party holds money beside a deficit; with two, the covered credit reads less than its cap admits (executed, P2 and P3; `probeA.sql`)

The per-credit cap admits `original − share as recorded − draws` (check_cash_source 3166–3180); the pool admits the family's total (3195–3225). Neither knows the cover. The cover puts a deficit on the latest credit holding money (1970–1981). So when a deficit sits beside **two** holders, the latest one's position drops by the deficit while its caps do not.

**P2 (owner history load, then tally_app).** Payment 100, K 100. K's refunds of 20 and 30 come back and are reissued as R1 (20) and R2 (30). K refunds its 50, R1 its 20, R2 its 30, all posted: nobody holds anything. 10 is taken back under the payment: the shortfall falls on K (share as recorded: K 10, R1 0, R2 0). Then R1's and R2's refund checks come back.

```
after the take-back:   payment 0, K −10, R1 0,  R2 0
after both returns:    payment 0, K 0,   R1 20, R2 20      (sum 40 = 100 − 10 − K's 50; right)
shares with the cover: K 0, R1 0, R2 10
agree():               FAIL (credit …23 = R2): it reads 20.00, and a refund of 20.01 got admitted
a refund of 30 from R2: admitted (its own cap 30; the pool 40); 30.01 refused (23514, its own cap)
a refund of 20.01 from R1: refused (23514)
after R2 refunds 30:   payment 0, K 0, R1 10, R2 0;  agree() passes
```

**P3 (the payment holds money too).** Payment 100, K 60; the payment's own 40 refunded; K's refund of 20 comes back as R; K refunds 40 and R its 20; 10 taken back (on K); the payment's refund comes back; R's refund comes back. Reads payment 40, K 0, R 10 (sum 50; right). agree() fails the same way: R reads 10, a refund of 10.01 is admitted, 20 is admitted. The payment reads 40 and 40.01 is refused. Had the payment covered first (the plan's other order), the payment would under-read by 10 instead: either order leaves one party reading less than its caps admit.

**What this is and is not.** No position reads more than its cap admits: a position is at most its own recorded figure, and the positive positions add up to the pool (predicted from the pass, executed in every shape). Money is safe; conservation holds; after any admitted draw the positions re-settle consistently (P2's last line). It is the comment at 1915–1917 ("A position then equals what its cap admits"), R17's "each side equals its cap", and agree()'s second leg that are false in general. Round 6's Opus B3 was this class (an under-read of 10 on the payment) and the plan took it as blocking, so I mark it the same, and say plainly it is a claim-and-test fix.

**Fix (recommended).** State the true invariant and test it: a position never exceeds what its caps admit; the payment's and the family's positions add up to what the ledger leaves; no credit reads money beside another credit's deficit, and no side beside the other's. agree() keeps "a refund of the position is admitted" and "the sum is the ledger", drops "a cent more is refused" for a credit when another family member also reads money (or replaces it with "a refund of the family's total plus a cent is refused"). Add P2 and P3 as checks. Fix the comment at 1915–1917, R17's last clause and R20. **Alternative:** the per-credit cap reads the covered position (then R2 may refund 20, R1 20, and the claim holds as written). It matches Kyle's "one pot" wording, but it makes a draw's cap depend on the reading instant and the cover, which r6 deliberately avoided ("a draw uses only its own side's money", 3159–3162). A drafting choice, not a ruling; I would narrow the claim.

### S2. A credit whose own money permanently covers a sibling's shortfall never reaches a terminal status (predicted; a limit to state in the header)

As built (the plan's point 2), a credit covering a deficit beside it "stays active, reading nothing, until the deficit goes". When the deficit is a bounce's shortfall that nothing will give back (the usual case), it never goes. Shape: K −10 (shortfall), R own 10 (its refund came back). R reads 0; a draw on R is refused by the pool (total 0); R's escheat is refused by the own-side check (it holds 10 of its own); R's void would need R to hold nothing drawn, which it does, so the void is admitted and returns 20... no: R's void is admitted only if R carries no share (it carries none) and returns its whole amount to K, which then reads 0 — so **the void is the only exit**, and it is a correction, not a closing. Financially the state is right (the utility holds R's 10 against the 10 the customer got; the customer is owed nothing; the state has no claim). The record just never closes: R active for ever, reading 0, with a live escheat clock the core must know to ignore (AC-44 says net first, so it would). **Fix:** say it under R22/R23 or a new residual, and in AC-44 that a credit reading 0 with money of its own is netted, not dormant. If a closing act is wanted later, it is "apply a credit to a shortfall under its own payment", a schema addition.
