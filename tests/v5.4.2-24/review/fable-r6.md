## N — notes

- **N1 (read, against -23's code).** The r6 plan's argument that the payment bound (`least(v_pay.amount, …)`) is unreachable is right. `enforce_ledger_reversal` holds, at every entry on the path from the new row to the root, the net of that entry's subtree between 0 and minus its amount, at every date. The rows under the posting entry therefore net to at most the payment, and a positive nsf row with its subtree nets to at least 0 (its own subtree lies in [−nsf, 0]), so leaving it out can only lower the sum. One more fact makes it doubly unreachable: `account_ledger_payment_arrival_key` is unique on the payment for posting rows, so the `e` anchor is always one entry, never two whose trees could add up. The guard stays as a guard; recording the mutation as equivalent is right.
- **N2 (executed, FG).** After the payment is `reversed`, the tree is fixed by -23 alone: give-backs are refused because the payment has ended, takes because the tree is already at its cap. The share and the last date the return is checked against cannot change under it.
- **N3 (executed, FM).** The tree-date rule is conservative when a positive nsf row is dated after the reversal: the bank's take-back does not move the misposted share, so a return dated between the reversal and the take-back would have balanced. The cost is a clerk's return date, never money; the gain is one walk and one rule to reason about. Fine as built; worth a line in the operator guidance at the core build (the message already names the date to use).
- **N4 (read).** The receipt dates a payment by `payment_date`; -23's `payment_position_as_of` returns no row before `payment_date` either. So the two sides agree even when the posting entry is dated earlier (P9b11's fixture: posted 03-10, dated 03-15), which -23 admits. The customer's running ledger balance by `transaction_date` does show the money earlier than either position function; that is -23's property (noted in the r5 plan, item 2), not -24's.
- **N5 (read; perf predicted from my r5 FE/FD).** The new date walk `t` has no depth bound of its own, like `down` and like -23's `ledger_tree_ids`: -22's lineage rule bounds every chain at insert (acyclic, ≤ 1000). It reads by `idx_account_ledger_reverses` under the function's planner settings, the same plan shape I measured in round 5 (20 ms over a 999-link chain for the share walk).
- **N6 (read).** M111 and M112 mutate what they claim and are named at the first check that fails for that reason alone (P14e: the nsf -50 under d4 is at depth 2, so the second leg's filter is the one that matters; P14h: the first refusal that depends on the tree date). All three walk anchors occur exactly once in the patch (`rep` asserts it). M111 is placed in the second leg only, rightly: a depth-1 nsf row is always positive (the sign rule), so the first leg's `amount > 0` has no shape that could kill it.
- **N7 (read).** Recorded instants: `check_suspense_recorded_order` reads the payment's `status_recorded_at` for a `reversed` payment, and -23 (`cash_lineage_recorded`) keeps that instant after every row of the lineage; so the return is recorded after the whole tree and after the payment ended. A read at an instant before the return sees the payment at 0 and the receipt not yet holding the money: an under-count, never a double.
